# EyesInOrbit reference

The detailed reference. For a short overview see the [README](../README.md).

Local space mission control. A Node aggregator polls public spaceflight feeds (CelesTrak, Launch Library 2, Spaceflight News, NASA DSN, JPL Horizons, wheretheiss.at, ISS Lightstreamer), caches them in SQLite, and streams one unified entity model to a three.js web client. Any tracked object can be watched live on the globe alongside its launch info, livestream, news and telemetry.

Monorepo (npm workspaces): `packages/core` (`@eio/core`, shared model), `apps/server` (`@eio/server`), `apps/web` (`@eio/web`).

## Features

- **Globe and tracking**: three.js scene with Earth, atmosphere, star field and terminator lighting. Thousands of satellites (SGP4 propagated in a Web Worker), stations, debris, launch pads, DSN ground stations and deep-space probes. Hover for a name tooltip, click to select, Follow keeps the camera on the selection, Esc resets. The Earth hides everything behind it (stars, satellites, paths, deep-space markers); picking ignores hidden points too.
- **Bodies are entities**: the Sun, the eight planets, Pluto and the dwarf planets Ceres, Eris, Haumea and Makemake, and 21 moons (the Moon, Phobos, Deimos, the Galilean moons, Mimas to Iapetus, the five large Uranus moons, Triton, Charon) are catalog entities (`body-sun`, `body-earth`, `body-mars`, `body-moon`, `body-jupiter`, `body-io`, `body-pluto`, ...; provider `body`, group `bodies`; kinds `star`, `planet`, `dwarf_planet`, `moon`) next to satellites and probes, so they show up in search and filters, have a position and an orbit trajectory (the Moon around the Earth), and carry `createdAt`. The server's `bodies` connector registers them offline; the web app and the mock build the same records from `bodyEntityRecords()`. Convention: [`CONTRACT.md`](CONTRACT.md#entity-conventions-server-produces-web-consumes).
- **Camera** (see [Controls](#controls)): altitude-aware zoom, a surface-aligned view (centred on the selected object, level horizon, north-up and steady over the poles; on by default near the Earth), and Fit (F) / Fit all (Shift+F) that frame the selection with its related objects or everything the filters leave on screen.
- **Trajectory paths**: select an object that is not SGP4 and its path is drawn: landing boosters (ascent and landing arc), launch vehicles with trajectory files, relative objects (`relative` provider), Keplerian objects and Horizons-sampled probes. Past is dim, future is bright, the current position is the selection ring, and composite phase boundaries (pad, ascent, landing, landed) get a tick and a label. Windows: probes -1 d to +2 d, trajectories -5 min to +10 min, relative objects +/-3 min, Keplerian -1/4 to +1 period. A launch vehicle's or booster's planned flight is drawn whole from the pad whatever the clock says; for a launch that goes on in its modeled orbit (`on orbit (modeled)`, 12 h) the line carries on past SECO only for the first part of the orbit (the window is capped at 45 minutes from liftoff, about 26 minutes of orbit for Falcon 9), because the whole path is one polyline glued to the rotating Earth; the full orbit is the orbit line of the vehicle once it is in orbit (it stops where the modeled orbit ends) and of its payload entity. The HUD shows the window. SGP4 objects keep their orbit line, trail and ground track.
- <a id="frame-of-reference"></a>**Frame of reference**: the scene is Earth-inertial, but the *frame* is a centre: an entity, a body (the Earth by default, the Moon, the Sun, Mars) or any other. What the frame changes is everything that depends on time: an object's trail and path are sampled relative to the centre at each sample time (`r(t) - c(t)`) and drawn around the centre's current position, so a Moon orbiter closes around the Moon, an Earth satellite's trail seen from the Moon is its true relative curve, and with the Earth (default) nothing differs from before. **Orbit lines** do not depend on the frame or the lock: a selected object's orbit line is drawn round what it orbits (an Earth satellite round the Earth, LRO round the Moon), and selecting a body (from the tree, the breadcrumb, the scene or the choice list: they all select the same way) also draws the orbits of its child bodies (the Earth: its own orbit round the Sun and the Moon's round the Earth, so the default Earth view shows both; the Sun: the Earth's and Mars's). All of them are drawn to scale, the Earth's and Mars's circles round the displayed Sun like the bodies, see [`docs/frames.md`](frames.md#orbit-line-rule). Readouts follow: the Telemetry tab shows range and speed relative to the centre (`Range from Moon`), and the orbit period uses the object's primary body (LRO: the Moon's gm, about 2 h). **One selection rule for every route** (tree rows and body nodes, the list, search results, the frame breadcrumb, a click in the scene, the choice list, the DSN table in the Data streams popup, launch cards, and the frame restored on reload): selecting an object selects it (the detail panel shows it), makes it the frame when it is a body (or an entity flagged orbitable) unless the frame is locked, turns Follow on and flies the camera there, once. Selecting any other object leaves the frame alone. Deselecting (the x in the detail panel) clears the selection and Follow and sends an unlocked frame back to the Earth; Esc does that, unlock the frame and take the camera home. There are no body tabs: bodies are chosen in the tree, the breadcrumb or the scene. The frame is remembered in this browser (`localStorage['eio.frame']`, the lock is not): after a reload it comes back once the catalog is loaded, (the previous format with a body's registry id is read too), falling back to the Earth when the stored centre no longer exists; the restored frame is selected and flown to like any selection. A locked frame (the lock toggle in the breadcrumb, or `Lock frame` in the detail panel; in code `setFrame(id, true)`) is never changed by selecting objects (the selection and the camera still go to the selected object, even a body), e.g. lock on the ISS, then pick other satellites to see their paths and range relative to the ISS (`Camera ... from ISS (locked)`). The entity that is the centre is drawn relative to its own primary body. Footprints and ground tracks stay Earth features. Design and contract: [`docs/frames.md`](frames.md); maths in `apps/web/src/lib/frame.ts`.
- **Search and filter**: one query drives everything (see [Search tags and hiding groups](#search-tags)): the typed text (name, NORAD or COSPAR id; ranked locally over the whole catalog, with the same ranking as `/api/search`), general category chips with counts (Satellites, Stations, Navigation, Probes, Launch vehicles, Debris, Ground, Bodies) and specific groups or launches through tags typed in the search field. The entity list, the tree counts, the scene (what is drawn, picked and labelled), Fit all and the 3D models all use the same predicate, so what is listed is what is drawn. Virtualised entity browser with a TREE / LIST toggle (see [Entity tree](#entity-tree)).
- <a id="search-tags"></a>**Search tags and hiding groups**: the query is a list of terms (`apps/web/src/lib/query.ts`): tags (`group:starlink`, `-kind:debris`) and the typed text, each with an effect, **only** (show only the matching objects; AND across keys, OR within a key, a leading `-` negates) or **hide** (the matching objects are not drawn and not listed; hide terms OR together). Every term is a chip in the search field (the text stays as typed): an eye on the chip switches it between only and hide, the x removes it, Backspace in an empty field removes the last chip. The category chips are sugar for `cat:` terms (chip: only, its eye: hide); `CLEAR` removes the chips, the text and the typed input but leaves the hidden groups, `SHOW ALL` (shown while something is hidden) removes the hide terms. The chips offer only general categories (`apps/web/src/lib/browser-ui.ts` `categoryOf`: every entity is in exactly one, from its kind, its group and `render.colorKey`; `Other` appears only when something is in it). Specific groups are tags: `#starlink` or `group:gps-ops` (a CelesTrak group; `group:cosmos` also matches `cosmos-2251-debris`), `kind:probe` (alias `type:`; `launch_vehicle`, `ground_station`...), `cat:navigation`, `launch:2024-123` (the launch group of the COSPAR designator; `launch:1993` is every launch of that year; accepting a launch from the suggestions also frames the camera on its members), `id:25544` (an entity id or an alias value: NORAD, COSPAR; aliases `norad:`, `cospar:`), `body:earth` (what orbits that body or one of its moons). An unknown `key:value` is plain text and a tag without a value yet (`group:`) is ignored; a tag word is applied once it is finished (a space, Enter or Tab after it). While typing `group:` or `#` (also `kind:`, `cat:`, `launch:`, `body:`) a suggestion list shows the matching names with counts: Up and Down move, Enter or Tab accept, Esc closes. The `?` button in the field lists the syntax. `classify(rec, query, ctx)` returns `off` (nothing restricts, drawn as usual), `show` (an only or text term lets it through), `out` (left out by an only or text term) or `hide` (a hide term); `ctx.focus` (the selection and the objects related to it) is never hidden or left out, the Sun, planets and moons are scenery and can never be hidden (they can still be filtered by an only term), and `ctx.text` holds the ids the typed text matches, looked up once per change in the store (`textIds`), not per frame. The tag terms with their effects are remembered in `localStorage['eio.query']` (the typed text is not); an old `localStorage['eio.hidden']` becomes hide terms once and is deleted. Backspace in the field removes the chip just before the typed text when there is nothing in front of the caret to delete (an empty field, or the caret at the very start of the text; a selection or a caret later in the text deletes text as usual; `backspaceRemovesChip`). Parsing and suggestions are pure and tested in `apps/web/src/lib/search-tags.ts`, the predicate and the term operations in `apps/web/src/lib/query.ts`.
- **Hide groups**: the eye next to a category chip, on a tag chip and on the tree's body and kind headers makes a hide term: those objects are not drawn (their point size is 0 like for a filtered-out object, so they cannot be picked, hovered, listed in the pick popup or labelled; their 3D models, orbit lines and footprints go too) and they are removed from the entity list and the tree, so every count matches what is drawn. A hidden chip is struck through (amber) in the search field; its eye shows only those objects instead, `SHOW ALL` removes every hide term. The selected object, and the objects related to it, stay drawn when the query would hide or filter them out, with no badge (it is in the scene and in the detail panel, just not in the list). Hiding is not a filter: it gives no labels to the rest and no scope exemption, it composes with the only and text terms and with the scope thinning. Not covered: launch pad markers (they are launches, not entities).
- **Highlight**: the Highlight item of the View menu, Scene section (off by default, remembered in `localStorage['eio.highlight']`). Off: what an only or text term leaves out is hidden. On: it is dimmed instead (smaller, fainter points), and the selected object, the objects related to it and what the query matches are drawn at full strength and a little larger. With a selection and no query, everything but the selection and its related objects is dimmed. Hide terms still hide.
- <a id="entity-tree"></a>**Entity tree**: the entity browser's default view (TREE; LIST is the flat list, the choice is remembered in this browser) groups objects under the body they orbit (`EntityRecord.bodyId`), nested like the bodies: `SUN > EARTH > (MOON > LRO, then the Earth's objects in kind groups: satellite, debris, ...)` and `SUN > MARS > rover`. Body nodes show how many objects they hold, below them and in their sub-bodies. Nodes start collapsed except the path to the frame of reference (the Earth: Sun and Earth open) and, while a search or filter is active, every node with a hit (the counts are those of the filtered set; bodies without a hit disappear; with no filter every body is a node, a childless planet too, and the moons stay folded under their planet). The arrow folds a node; the body nodes are the body entities (`body-sun`, `body-earth`, `body-mars`, `body-moon`): a click on a body name selects the body (the detail panel shows its radius, GM, orbital period and parent), makes it the frame of reference and flies there (Earth, Moon, Sun and Mars alike); a click on an object selects it exactly as in the list; the object that is the frame is tagged `FRAME`; each body and kind header carries an eye that hides what is under it (not drawn, not listed). Selecting an object anywhere else (the globe, the pick popup, a label, a launch board card, a path, the breadcrumb) reveals it in the browser: the nodes above its row open (and stay open, like your own), the list scrolls so the row is centred when it was out of view, and the row is highlighted; in LIST it just scrolls. A click on a row in the browser never moves the list, and a search or filter that hides the object is left as it is (nothing is revealed). While you scroll the tree, the ancestors of the first row in view stay pinned at the top like an IDE outline (`SUN`, `EARTH`, the kind group; `stickyAncestors` in `lib/entity-tree.ts`, read from the scroll position once per animation frame, at most half the list's height): clicking a pinned header does what clicking that node does, and a row revealed by a selection is scrolled below them, never under them. Built on the virtualised list: only open nodes become rows (one flattened array, one fixed-height row each, pure logic in `apps/web/src/lib/entity-tree.ts`), so the 12,000-object catalog costs one pass per change and a few dozen DOM rows.
- <a id="colour-legend"></a>**Colour coding**: an object's colour is its general category (the chips of the entity browser, `categoryOf` in `apps/web/src/lib/browser-ui.ts`), the same in the scene's points, the tree and list dots, the category chips, the colour legend (bottom left of the scene, above the readouts; View menu, Scene, Colour legend) and the stripe on an object label, so there is no per-constellation colour: Starlink is a satellite like the other Earth satellites (the group is still a `group:starlink` tag away). The nine colours are defined once in `CATEGORIES` (`browser-ui.ts`, mirrored as `--c-<category>` in `styles.css` and read by `scene/palette.ts`): Satellites `#90bef1` (light blue), Stations `#f9f684` (pale yellow), Navigation `#ff58a6` (pink), Probes `#7781f2` (violet), Launch vehicles `#fb9f21` (orange), Debris `#777d7f` (grey), Ground `#40b272` (green), Bodies `#e8f5fd` (white), Other `#3eeec1` (aqua). Each has a contrast of at least 4.8:1 against the scene background `#05080c` and 4.6:1 against the panels, and every pair stays apart under normal, protan, deutan and tritan vision (`apps/web/test/palette.test.ts`). The selection, hover and highlight treatments are unchanged. `render.colorKey` in the entity record is advisory: the server still sets it (`starlink`, `navigation`, ...), the web only reads it as a hint when the kind does not decide the category.
- **Frame breadcrumb and lock**: the frame bar at the top left of the scene shows the frame as a path from the Sun (`FRAME  SUN > EARTH > MOON > LRO`). Click a crumb to select it (it becomes the frame unless locked) and fly there; a crumb whose entity is not in the catalog is greyed. The lock toggle (`Lock` / `Locked`, amber when locked) keeps the frame while other objects are selected, e.g. lock the ISS and pick satellites to see their paths and range relative to it. In the selection panel, the `Lock frame` key (under the name) makes the selected object the frame and locks it (it reads `Frame locked` while it is the locked frame). When the frame is an object with no state at the simulation time (outside its data range) the bar says so, because paths relative to it are then not drawn.
- **Launches in the search**: `launch:` tags select the objects that share a launch (derived from the COSPAR designator `YYYY-NNN`; `launch:1993` is a year). The suggestion list for `launch:` shows the launch groups (objects sharing a launch, only groups with 2 or more members, largest first, with their label and count; type `launch:2251`, `launch:cosmos` or `launch:1993-036`); accepting one adds the chip and frames the camera on its members through `runtime.onFitRequest`. Use the chip's eye to hide the launch instead, or HIGHLIGHT to dim everything else rather than hide it. Example: `launch:cosmos`, accept COSMOS 2251, to see its debris cloud at once.
- **Detail panel** with tabs:
  - Telemetry: lat/lon/altitude, geocentric range, speed, period, phase, element age, confidence, attributes, identifiers; ISS live fix and telemetry; the current DSN link for DSN-tracked probes.
  - <a id="about"></a>About: a description of the selected object, looked up by the server only when the tab is open (`GET /api/describe/:entityId`, `ui/detail/AboutTab.tsx`). Shows the Wikipedia summary with a small thumbnail and links to Wikipedia, Wikidata and ESA DISCOS; an ESA DISCOS facts table (class, mass, shape, size, span, operator, status) when a DISCOS token is set (Data streams, ESA DISCOS, or `DISCOS_TOKEN`; without one DISCOS is skipped and the feed shows OFF, needs credentials); and always one line composed from the entity's own catalogue fields (kind, group, launch year from the COSPAR designator, ids) with a Wikipedia search link. Lookup: Wikidata item by satellite catalogue number (P377) through `list=search&srsearch=haswbstatement:P377=<NORAD>`; several items share a number (the ISS and its Zarya module), so the one whose COSPAR id (P8913) matches wins, then an exact label match, and otherwise none is shown rather than a guess; its English sitelink goes to the Wikipedia REST `page/summary`. A probe without a NORAD number is matched by exact English label only if exactly one Wikidata item with that label is described as a spacecraft; bodies use their name as the article title; other kinds get the fallback line only. Scalable by construction: nothing is fetched in bulk, only for the object whose tab is open; answers are cached in SQLite (hit 30 days, miss 7 days, a lookup with a source error 10 minutes), concurrent asks for one object share one lookup, each request is a `demand` call through the request limiter (Wikimedia 1.5 s apart, 600 per hour; DISCOS 10 per minute; see [Rate limits](#rate-limits)), a source has a persistent failure gate (`backoff.ts`), and `OFFLINE=1` serves the cache or the fallback line. The sources show in the Data streams popup as `wikipedia` (Wikidata and Wikipedia) and `discos`. The DISCOS parser was written from ESA's documented v2 JSON:API shape without a token: its fixture is docs-shaped placeholder data, not a live response. Mock mode (`?mock=1`) returns the real ISS summary text (the recorded fixture) with DISCOS off (needs a token) and only the fallback line for every other object. Links and pictures are shown only for https URLs on wikipedia.org, wikimedia.org, wikidata.org and discosweb.esoc.esa.int (checked in the parser and again in `AboutTab`). Nothing queues or sleeps: when the limiter refuses a request the source state is `limited`, `limitedUntil` carries the retry time, the description is not cached and the tab says "rate limited, try again at HH:MMZ"; the browser reuses an answer (or the lookup in flight) for 60 s per object and hides a thumbnail that fails to load, without retrying. A lookup made under `OFFLINE=1` is never cached; changing the DISCOS token clears its failure gate and asks again.
  - Launch (launch entities only): countdown, status, phase, window, provider, vehicle, mission, pad, and for Falcon 9 an [ascent telemetry](#ascent-telemetry) section (modeled or measured altitude, speed and downrange charts, event timeline).
  - Stream (when the entity or its launch has streams): YouTube, Twitch, Vimeo and direct video links (.mp4, .webm, .m3u8) are embedded; NASA TV, X and other links open in a new tab. POP OUT floats the stream above the app (up to 4 windows; drag the title bar, resize from the corner at 16:9, × closes it; while it is popped out the Stream tab offers DOCK BACK, which closes it and plays the stream in the tab again; positions are remembered). Hover a YouTube player for PLAY, PAUSE, the volume button and CONTROLS (Twitch, Vimeo and video files keep their own controls, volume included); the volume button shows 10 bars, light up to the volume, all dark when muted: click it to mute or unmute (the level is remembered), scroll the wheel over it to change the volume in 10% steps (wheel up on a muted player unmutes it; the page and the globe do not scroll or zoom meanwhile; Up/Right and Down/Left arrows on the focused button do the same; at 0% a click raises it to 10%), logic in `lib/volume.ts`; the YouTube embeds hide what YouTube's parameters allow (see `apps/web/src/lib/youtube.ts`). CONTROLS switches every YouTube player, docked and popped out, to YouTube's own control bar (seek, quality, captions, fullscreen); the players reload once, HIDE CONTROLS in the corner switches back, and the choice is remembered in this browser.
  - News: Spaceflight News articles for the object (`links.newsQuery` or name), or all spaceflight.
- **Launch board**: upcoming launches (plus the last 2 hours) with live countdowns, LL2 status and derived mission phase. Click a card to select the launch entity or fly to its pad. The small SKIP button on a card (and SKIP TO LAUNCH in the Launch tab) jumps the simulation clock to liftoff, see [Jumping to a launch](#jumping-to-a-launch).
- <a id="observatories"></a>**Observatories panel and Sky view**: the bottom row, beside the launch board (View > Panels > Observatories; its width has its own splitter when both panels are shown, sizes and visibility are remembered), lists every entity that carries the `observatory` attribute: JWST, Hubble (CelesTrak NORAD 20580), Chandra and TESS, the three DSN complexes (radio, no sky imagery) and the ground sites Mauna Kea, Paranal (VLT) and Rubin (Cerro Pachon), with site type (ground or space), location and wavebands. A row selects the entity (`selectObject`); `type:observatory` in the search field finds them all. **LOOK FROM HERE** is the first-person view (`SceneController.lookFrom`, rig mode `firstPerson`, maths in `apps/web/src/lib/first-person.ts`): the main scene camera replaces the Earth view and stands AT the selected observatory, a ground site 2 m above its geodetic position (and never below the camera's 3 km altitude floor, so Paranal and Rubin are lifted a few hundred metres, and Mauna Kea is 4.2 km up), a space telescope where it is now, and looks outward in the site's local east/north/up frame with a level horizon (a telescope has no horizon: it sees the sky and the Earth below). Left drag turns the view (azimuth is a compass bearing, elevation up to 86 degrees either way, the sky follows the pointer), the wheel and pinch change the field of view from 1 to 120 degrees (it starts at 60), pan is off. The scene readout shows `Looking from Mauna Kea Observatories, az 120 deg el 15 deg, FOV 60 deg` instead of the camera distance. The observatory's own point, ring, label and model are not drawn. It is entered with the usual mode transition and left by Esc, the **Leave** button in the scene toolbar, selecting anything else, Follow off, or Fit / Model; Esc and Leave use the home route (the selection is cleared) and the camera, its field of view and the view distance, azimuth and elevation come back as they were before. S is ignored meanwhile. The Sky view window does not follow the first-person view. SKY VIEW (or a double-click on a row) opens a floating window (it shares the four-window cap with the stream windows) with Aladin Lite looking from the selected observatory: a ground site starts at its zenith (RA = local sidereal time, Dec = latitude, `apps/web/src/lib/sky-frame.ts`) and, while FOLLOW ZENITH is on, is re-aimed each 5 s step of sim time; a space telescope starts at the Galactic centre because no pointing data is used (the window says so). Drag and zoom are Aladin's own, but the view is gnomonic (TAN, what a camera sees, not Aladin's default ball-shaped orthographic projection) with the field of view capped at 100 degrees (`MAX_FOV_DEG`: TAN stretches the edges without bound toward 180), and dragging or zooming switches FOLLOW ZENITH off so the next 5 s step does not snap the view back. The window's resize grip stays above the Sky view's bar (`.float-body` isolates its children's z-indexes) and a move or resize is followed by window-level pointer listeners, not by pointer capture alone. Surveys (HiPS, CDS ids): DSS2 colour, 2MASS, Pan-STARRS DR1, Fermi, ROSAT all-sky (X-ray); there is no Chandra HiPS, ROSAT stands in for X-ray. Aladin Lite 3.8.2 is loaded on first use from `https://cdn.jsdelivr.net/npm/aladin-lite@3.8.2/dist/aladin.js` (not an npm dependency, no integrity hash; `apps/web/src/lib/aladin.ts` is the only code that touches it); offline, the window says it could not load. Credits: Aladin Lite and the HiPS surveys are from the CDS, Strasbourg (https://aladin.cds.unistra.fr/AladinLite/); the survey data belong to their projects (DSS2/STScI, 2MASS, Pan-STARRS, Fermi, ROSAT). Site coordinates and their sources are cited in `packages/core/src/observatory.ts` (Mauna Kea and Rubin from Wikipedia, Paranal from ESO). **Remote loading**: opening the Sky view loads Aladin Lite 3.8.2 from cdn.jsdelivr.net, and Aladin then fetches survey properties and tiles from the first server of the survey that answers (`lib/hips-mirror.ts`: CDS alasky first, then the mirrors listed in the survey's CDS MocServer record, IRSA, ESA and CASDA; one `properties` probe per server per survey per session, a survey with no working server is retried after 5 minutes and shows "unavailable"; since 2026-11-12 CDS alasky answers every survey with a 404 page, so DSS2, 2MASS and Fermi come from mirrors and Pan-STARRS and ROSAT, which have none, are unavailable), so those requests reveal the sky position being viewed (and the survey) to the CDS; the Aladin module runs in the page without a subresource-integrity hash, so the page trusts that CDN file. Aladin's own usage logging is switched off (`log: false`). Aladin gives its host element `position: relative` and no height, which collapsed the Sky view to 0 px (black window); `.sky > .sky-aladin.sky-aladin` in `styles.css` keeps the host filling the window. Nothing is requested until the Sky view is opened.
- **Watch a livestream**: paste any link into the **Watch a livestream** field (footer of the Data streams popup; there is no separate Watch button) and press Enter: it opens in a pop-out window, and the field can be used again for up to 4 streams at once. `lib/stream-url.ts` (`embedFor`) resolves the link: YouTube (watch, live, shorts, embed, youtu.be, channel live), Twitch (`player.twitch.tv`, channel or video, `parent` = the page's host), Vimeo (`player.vimeo.com/video/<id>`) and direct .mp4, .webm or .m3u8 files (a `<video>` element; HLS plays only in browsers that support it natively, such as Safari). Only http(s) links are accepted (`javascript:`, `data:`, `file:` and links with credentials are refused); anything else shows an inline reason and, for a web page, an Open in new tab link. The Stream tab's POP OUT uses the same resolver. Not verified against the real providers (embeds were only checked as URLs in mock mode).
- **Data streams popup**: the top bar **Data streams** button is the one data-connection control. It shows the link state (dot and Link up / Connecting / Reconnecting / Offline / Mock data) and the `N failing` / `N degraded` chips; below 960 px the state word is dropped and the dot and chips remain. The popup has a status line on top (state, loaded object count, server time), then lists every data feed on the left with its health (dot and OK/WARN/FAIL/OFF badge, worst first) and shows the chosen feed on the right: what it provides, who publishes it (link), status in words, last OK, next run, last error, and a feed-specific section: the **Deep Space Network** table on the DSN feed, and the **API keys and logins** form on `spacetrack`, `launchlibrary` and `discos` (`ui/CredentialsSection.tsx`; which feed uses which connector is `credentialConnector` in `lib/feed-catalog.ts`). The descriptions are static text in `lib/feed-catalog.ts` (every server and mock connector id has an entry, tested). The **Deep Space Network** table lives here (select the `dsn` feed): dishes with active links first, craft chips that select the spacecraft, range, round-trip light time, downlink band and rate (`ui/DsnSection.tsx`). It used to be a bottom-row panel; that slot is free, and an older stored `dsn` panel key is ignored. The last row, **Sky imagery (HiPS)**, is not a server connector: it says the Sky view's library (jsDelivr) and survey tiles (IRSA, ESA, CASDA, CDS) are fetched by the browser, and which server each opened survey currently uses (`MirrorPicker.current`). Esc or a click outside closes the popup. The DSN data keeps updating while it is closed, and the entity detail's Telemetry tab still shows a probe's current DSN link.
- **View menu**: the top bar button opens a popover with two sections. Scene: Labels, Highlight, Close-up Earth imagery, Draw everything (off = auto scope) and Colour legend. Panels: Launches and News ticker. A hidden panel gives its space back (the globe grows; with Launches hidden the whole bottom row goes), and showing it again restores its previous size. The choice is kept in `localStorage` (`eio.panels.v1`); defaults: Launches and ticker shown. The Scene choices are remembered as before (scope resets on reload).
- **News ticker**: scrolling global headlines, pauses on hover.
- **Time controls**: Live / Go live, play/pause and rewind icon keys (rewind runs time backwards at the selected speed), speeds ×1 to ×600 and the offset from now (`T+dd d hh:mm:ss`, next to the clock; editable as four numeric fields) that show and set the offset of sim time from now (see [Controls](#controls)). All providers are evaluated at the simulation clock. Data windows limit what exists away from now: SGP4 objects extrapolate (accuracy falls off over days), Horizons probes cover -3 d to +7 d around the last fetch, and an entity without a state at the chosen time shows "No state available at this time" with its data range in the Telemetry tab.
- **Scope (what is drawn)**: the scene does not draw everything everywhere. With scope auto (default; Draw everything off) an object is drawn when it is within 80 camera distances of the camera focus (the camera target: the followed object, the surface point, or the Earth's centre), with a floor of 100 km: 4 km above a pad that keeps ~320 km around it (the pad complex, the booster; not the satellite catalogue or any probe), the default Earth view (24,000 km) keeps ~1.9 million km (JWST yes; the farther probes appear once the camera pulls out a little). Past the radius the satellite cloud is thinned over one more radius (a stable pseudo-random subset) and single entities are cut at 1.5 radii. Always kept: the selection, its related objects (the same lookup Fit uses), the hovered object and, while an only or text term is on, everything the search lets through. Objects behind the Earth are hidden exactly (see Draw order and depth), and out-of-scope objects can be neither hovered nor picked. The Draw everything item of the View menu toggles auto / all (all draws everything the search allows), and the HUD shows `n of total drawn` (in scope, not filtered out, inside the view and in front of the Earth, out of everything with a position). Policy maths: `lib/scope.ts`.
- **Labels**: names drawn over the scene (the Labels item of the View menu, on by default, remembered in `localStorage['eio.labels']`). Bodies (Sun, Earth, Moon, Mars: the body entities with `render.label: true`) are named when zoomed out: the label sits beside the disc, is full while the body's apparent radius is under 50 px, fades out linearly to nothing at 220 px (a body that fills the view needs no name, so the default 24,000 km Earth view has no Earth label) and is hidden when the body is behind the Earth, Moon, Sun or Mars. The selected object is always named (an object whose icon is hidden over a small, zoomed-out body, see Click below, gets no label either); other entities are named only if they ask for it (`render.label: true`: the ISS, probes, launch vehicles, DSN stations), unless the search has an only or text term (typed text or a tag), in which case the objects it lets through are named too (satellite labels need such a search; hidden groups give none). Priority: the selection, the hovered object, bodies, `render.label` entities, then the satellites the search lets through, nearest the screen centre first (a label shown last frame keeps its place against a slightly nearer rival). At most 48 labels, a label overlapping a better one is dropped, names are cut at 28 characters. Candidates for the searched satellites are re-scanned about 7 times a second (the 96 nearest the screen centre per point set); every frame only those few are projected. The planner (`apps/web/src/lib/labels.ts`) is pure and tested; the DOM nodes are pooled (`apps/web/src/scene/LabelLayer.ts`), only changed properties are written.
- **Everything is drawn to scale**: there is no distance compression. Probes are drawn at their real geocentric positions (Voyager 1 at ~2.5e10 km, 167 AU), their paths at true scale (Voyager's 3 days are ~4.4 million km, a few pixels at most from far out, visible once you zoom in), the Sun at 1.5e8 km and Mars at its real distance with its orbiters around it, and the markers keep their screen size. The camera may zoom out to 1e11 km. Selecting a probe or Fit / Fit all frame at those distances. The floating origin (positions are float64, camera-relative on the GPU) and the logarithmic depth buffer (far plane 1e13 km) carry the range; the near clamp (3 km above the ground, 0.2 km from a followed object) is unchanged. A log-compressed "deep-space shell" and a DISTANCE (COMPRESSED / TRUE SCALE) button used to exist; they are gone, so Mars and the probes orbiting it are always in the same frame and scale.
- **ISS**: while the ISS is selected the client subscribes to the `iss` WebSocket topic and the server starts a wheretheiss.at fix every 2 s (lat/lon/alt/velocity/visibility/footprint) and a Lightstreamer ISSLIVE telemetry feed (solar array joint angles, attitude quaternion, state vector). Both connectors are idle otherwise. See [ISS array telemetry](#iss-array-telemetry) for exactly what is verified.
- **3D models** (`apps/web/src/scene/models/`): entities whose `render.model` names a registered model are drawn as a true-size procedural three.js model instead of a point once the camera is near: `iss` (the integrated truss 108 m across with eight solar array wings on SARJ and BGA joints, radiators, the pressurized stack, Canadarm2; 73 m long), `satellite` (bus, two sun-tracking panels; one mock Starlink uses it) and `rocket-stage` (Falcon 9 first-stage proportions, legs, grid fins; launch boosters use it). There are no binary assets. Models are placed camera-relative in float64 like everything else (floating origin, log depth), so they stay steady at 25 m from the camera. They appear within 45 km (ISS), 12 km (stage) or 6 km (satellite); the point stays until the model is larger than it on screen (about 9 km for the ISS), then the point and, for a large model, the selection ring give way to the model; the model keeps the entity's picking, hover, scope and filter behaviour (a filter-dimmed entity gets a dimmed model). Orientation is computed from the state vector: the flight attitude LVLH (x along the velocity, z nadir, y completing) for stations and satellites, nose along the Earth-relative velocity for stages (upright on the pad and while landing); it rotates correctly in the inertial frame while you scrub. Lighting is the Sun direction plus a little ambient and earthshine, darkened by a conical Earth-shadow model. With no model in range the cost is one distance test per model entity. When the ISS arrays are live, the measured attitude quaternion replaces the plain LVLH attitude.
- **ISS solar arrays**: the model's two SARJ and eight BGA joints are driven by the live ISSLIVE angles when that is verified, fresh and the clock is live, else by a computed pose that points the arrays at the Sun (derived from the Sun vector in the ISS body frame). The Telemetry tab shows the source (`LIVE`, `COMPUTED` or `STATIC`) and an ARRAYS readout (SARJ port and starboard, the eight BGA angles); the HUD shows `Model ISS, arrays ...`. Mock mode animates the arrays with the computed pose.
- **Connection state and feed health**: shown on the **Data streams** top bar button (link state and failing/degraded chips) and in its popup (per-feed status, age and errors); there is no separate health popover.
- **Offline behaviour**: `OFFLINE=1` serves only what the SQLite cache holds. `npm run assets` downloads the Earth map `earth-hires.jpg` (NASA Blue Marble resized from the 21600 x 10800 original to 8192 px, 4.9 km per texel, about 180 MB of video memory; a GPU with a smaller texture limit gets it scaled down to that limit) and the Moon and Mars maps (`mars.jpg`: the Viking MDIM 2.1 colour mosaic from the USGS Astrogeology WMS, 4096 x 2048, about 1.5 MB, which is the largest size that server returns; `MARS_URL=<any equirectangular JPEG url> npm run assets` swaps it). It is the only Earth map: close views add the streamed DETAIL tiles, and there is no 2k fallback. The map is loaded once at page load, so reload the page after downloading it. The browser console says which map was used or why it was skipped. If you skip `npm run assets` (or the file is missing) the globe uses the procedural Earth, the Moon a grey noise surface and Mars the tinted sphere (the console says so). A stale `apps/web/public/textures/earth.jpg` from earlier versions is no longer read; it is git-ignored and can be deleted.
- **Mock mode**: add `?mock=1` to the web URL to run entirely on synthetic in-memory data (also used automatically when `/api/health` is unreachable). `?mock=1&n=12000` sets the number of mock satellites (default 2000, at most 2,000,000) for stress tests, and `&perf=1` shows a performance readout (frame time, propagation, store ingest, search and tree build, heap); measurements and a ranked plan for scaling: [`docs/perf.md`](perf.md).

## Quick start

Requires **Node 24** (the SQLite cache uses the built-in `node:sqlite`).

```
npm install
npm run assets     # optional: downloads the Earth (8192 px), Moon and Mars maps (procedural Earth, plain Moon and tinted Mars are used without them)
npm run dev        # server on :8787, web on :5173
npm test           # vitest, all workspaces
```

Open http://localhost:5173. The Vite dev server proxies `/api` and `/stream` to `localhost:8787` (set by `EIO_SERVER`, default `localhost:8787`, in `apps/web/vite.config.ts`; start Vite with `EIO_SERVER=localhost:8799` for a server on another `PORT`).

Other scripts: `npm run build` (production build of the web app into `apps/web/dist`; the server does not serve it), `npm run typecheck`, `npm run start -w @eio/server` (server without file watching).

### Amateur and navigation satellites

- **Navigation (MEO)**: CelesTrak groups `gps-ops` (GPS operational; there is no group named `gps`), `glo-ops` (GLONASS), `galileo`, `beidou` are fetched like the other groups and shown in the navigation colour (legend "Navigation"). They are medium-Earth-orbit sets (about 12 to 14 h periods), which satellite.js propagates with SDP4; core tests cover a GPS-like and a GEO set. CelesTrak also offers one combined `gnss` group (one download instead of four) that is not used because the constellations are separate chips.
- **Adding groups raises 403 exposure**: each group is one more download from CelesTrak, which asks clients not to fetch every group and blocks repeated downloads. Each group keeps its own cache (TTL 3 h) and its own backoff gate, so a blocked group never delays the others, and the five new groups are fetched last. For every new group a hand-saved `data/celestrak/<group>.json` (from `https://celestrak.org/NORAD/elements/gp.php?GROUP=<group>&FORMAT=json`) works as the fallback. If the extra downloads are a problem, remove groups from `FETCH_ORDER` in `connectors/celestrak.ts`.
- **Amateur radio**: CelesTrak group `amateur` (about 100 objects) gives the element sets; they stay in the plain satellite colour and are selected through their group. The group names were checked against third-party listings of CelesTrak's current-data page (`galileo`, `gps-ops` also on CelesTrak's own pages); CelesTrak itself could not be reached from the research environment.
- **SatNOGS DB** (<https://db.satnogs.org/api/>, CC BY-SA 4.0): read access needs no token. `GET /api/satellites/?format=json` lists satellites (`sat_id`, `norad_cat_id`, `name`, `names`, `status` such as `in orbit` or `re-entered`, `launched`, `operator`, `countries`, `reception_status`); `GET /api/transmitters/?format=json&satellite__norad_cat_id=<id>` lists frequencies and modes (`description`, `alive`, `uplink_low`, `downlink_low`, `mode`, `status`, `service`); `/api/tle/` serves element sets but CelesTrak already provides these and also has a `satnogs` group of the same objects. Anonymous throttling is not documented for DB (the SatNOGS Network observations API allows 60 requests per hour anonymous, 240 with a token). A connector that adds transmitter/status `attrs` to the `amateur` entities is not built: the hub replaces whole records on upsert, so the celestrak connector would have to merge the SatNOGS data itself. Other feeds: N2YO needs an API key and has its own request limits; AMSAT's status pages are HTML only.

## Rate limits

Every outgoing request of the server and its scripts passes one persistent limiter (`apps/server/src/net/limiter.ts`, the table in `net/limits.ts`, entry points `guardedFetch` and `guardedWebSocket` in `net/guarded-fetch.ts`). `EIO_RATE_LIMITS=off` switches the limiter off only under the test runner (outside it the variable is ignored with a startup warning). `test/net-guard.test.ts` fails when `fetch(`, any `fetch*(` call, `new WebSocket`, or a (dynamic) import of http, https, http2, net, tls, undici or ws appears anywhere else (apart from the run-once `scripts/fetch-assets.mjs`). State per source lives in the SQLite cache (`limit:<source>`) and is written **before** the request is sent, so a crash still counts the attempt and a restart never resets anything: minimum spacing (`nextAllowedAt`), minute, hour and day counters (per attempt) and `blockedUntil`. A block is set from HTTP 403/429/503 (per source), `Retry-After` (seconds or an HTTP date, at most 24 h), `X-Rate-Limit-*` headers, repeated failures and an exponential backoff where the table has one. The older per-source retry gates in `backoff.ts` (`backoff:<source>` keys) still run next to it for the connectors that had them.

Two modes. A `poll` call (a scheduled connector) may sit out a short spacing gap (up to 15 s; ISS Lightstreamer never waits) and otherwise the connector is rescheduled to the retry time; a spacing refusal is silent, a cap or a block set by a rate signal (403/429/Retry-After/X-Rate-Limit) shows as an orange "rate limited until HH:MMZ" in the Data streams popup, while a wait caused by our own failure backoff (DSN, network errors, repeated failures) keeps the normal failure mapping, red "repeated" included (`health.limit`, `classifyFeed` reason `rate-limited`). A `demand` call (the About tab, a SNAPI search, an imagery tile) never queues and never sleeps for a cap or block: it answers from the cache or says `limited` at once (About: `sources.*: 'limited'` plus `limitedUntil`, not cached, shown as "rate limited, try again at HH:MMZ"; `/api/news` and `/api/imagery`: HTTP 429 with `Retry-After`). A lookup may wait up to 2 s for the spacing gap of its own follow-up request (Wikidata, then Wikipedia) and for a tile (1 s), nothing more.

| Source (limiter key) | Published limit | What this app enforces |
|---|---|---|
| CelesTrak (`celestrak`) | [usage policy](https://celestrak.org/usage-policy.php): one download per group per 3 h, 403 for clients that repeat | one download per group per 3 h (cache TTL), at most 15 requests per hour in total, 2 s between requests, HTTP 403/429 blocks 3 h (longer with Retry-After), 3 failed HTTP answers within 2 h (not network errors or timeouts, so a flapping connection never triggers it) stop it for 24 h; `celestrak:seed` and `celestrak:probe` use the same state |

Routes, query parameters, message types and entity conventions are specified in [`CONTRACT.md`](CONTRACT.md); shared types are in `packages/core/src/{types,feeds}.ts`.

### API keys and logins in the UI

The **Data streams** popup holds the optional credentials, in the pane of the feed that uses them (Space-Track: username and password; Launch Library: token): the Space-Track username and password and the Launch Library 2 token. Saving sends them to the local server (`PUT /api/credentials`, accepted from localhost only), which keeps them in `data/cache.sqlite` (plain text, like `.env`; the file is git-ignored) and uses them for its own requests. They take effect immediately, without a restart: a connector that was waiting for credentials starts and runs once, and changing the Space-Track login drops its cached elements and retry gate so the new login is tried at once. Values typed here override `.env`; clearing one falls back to `.env`. Secrets are write-only: `GET /api/credentials` reports only whether one is set and where it came from (`ui` or `env`), and the username is the one value shown back. The browser never calls Space-Track or Launch Library itself, because those services do not allow browser-origin requests with a login.

## Trajectory files

Drop ascent profiles into `data/trajectories/` as `*.json` or `*.csv`. The server rescans every 30 s; a matching launch becomes a `composite` entity (geodetic at the pad until NET, then a `sampled` ascent in `EARTH:fixed`). Files with errors are skipped and reported in the `flightclub` connector note. `*.local.*` files are git-ignored. A working example is `data/trajectories/example.json` (illustrative, matches no real launch).

JSON:

```json
{
  "id": "my-ascent",
  "source": "free text",
  "azimuthDeg": 90,
  "match": { "launchId": "<LL2 launch uuid>", "nameContains": "Starlink" },
  "rows": [
    { "tSec": 0,  "altKm": 0,  "speedKmS": 0.0 },
    { "tSec": 60, "altKm": 9,  "speedKmS": 0.45 }
  ]
}
```

CSV: `# key: value` comment lines (`id`, `source`, `azimuthDeg`, `launchId`, `nameContains`), then a header `tSec,altKm,speedKmS[,downrangeKm]` and one row per sample.

Rules (see the format comment at the top of `apps/server/src/trajectory.ts`):
- `match` needs `launchId` (exact LL2 id) or `nameContains` (case-insensitive substring of the launch name); `launchId` is tried first.
- At least 2 rows; `tSec` is seconds since liftoff, anchored at the launch NET.
- Every row needs either `downrangeKm`, or `speedKmS` (downrange is then integrated from speed and altitude rate).
- `azimuthDeg` defaults to 90 (due east).

## Jumping to a launch

All three only move the shared simulation clock (`clock.seek`); nothing is loaded, so they work for past and future launches alike, and a launch whose entity has no data at that time simply shows nothing in the scene.

- **SKIP TO LAUNCH** (launch board card: `SKIP`; Launch tab): seek to T-0 of the launch, its NET as the server last published it (read at the click, so a hold or a new NET is followed; a TBD / TBC launch is jumped to its placeholder NET, which the tooltip says). It plays from there: live and paused become x1, a forward scrub keeps its speed, a rewind turns forward.
- **Event rows and charts** (Launch tab, ascent telemetry): clicking a row of the events table, an event label on the timeline, or anywhere in a chart or the timeline jumps to `liftoff + T+`. The chart click is clamped to the charted range. These keep the rate: paused stays paused, a rewind keeps rewinding, live becomes x1.
- **The trajectory in the scene** (see the Click row of the controls table): the time of the nearest point of the drawn ascent or booster-return path, interpolated between samples.

Pure logic: `apps/web/src/lib/launch-seek.ts` (T-0, event time, rates, tooltips), `lib/path-pick.ts` (nearest point of a polyline in screen space, perspective-correct, clipped at the camera plane); the clock access is `state/seek.ts`.

## Ascent telemetry

Launch entities of **Falcon 9** launches (not Falcon Heavy) and **Electron** launches get an ascent path and an event timeline even though no open ascent telemetry exists (reference doc, section 2). Three sources, in order of precedence, reported as `attrs.trajectorySource`:

| `trajectorySource` | What it is | Measured? |
|---|---|---|
| `live` | rows posted to `POST /api/telemetry/:launchId` | yes, as accurate as whoever posts them |
| `file` | a file in `data/trajectories/` (Flight Club export, hand-made) | no |
| `modeled` | the parametric nominal ascent in `packages/core/src/ascent-model.ts` | **no, a model** |

**Modeled ascent.** Built from typical Falcon 9 / Starlink webcast figures: max-Q at about T+1:10 (12 km, 0.42 km/s), MECO at about T+2:30 (68 km, 2.3 km/s Earth-relative), stage separation 3 s and second-stage ignition 8 s after MECO, fairing at T+3:30, SECO-1 at about T+8:30 (165 km, about 7.6 km/s), booster entry burn, landing burn and touchdown (droneship about T+8:20, RTLS about T+7:45; the recovery plan comes from LL2 `launcher_stage[0].landing`). The launch azimuth follows from the target inclination and pad latitude (`sin Az = cos i / cos lat`, southward branch for Vandenberg, corrected for the Earth's rotation); the inclination comes from the launch description, a per-Starlink-group table (group 15 is the 70 degree shell) or a per-orbit and per-site default, and the used value is shown in the Launch tab. Downrange is integrated from speed and altitude rate with `reconstructDownrange`. After SECO the state is held for 10 minutes (a placeholder; the real orbit rises towards apogee, phase `orbit (held)`, until T+1110 s for Falcon 9, where the mission phase also turns ON_ORBIT). From there the vehicle follows a two-body orbit through that state for about 12 hours (phase `on orbit (modeled)`; no J2, no drag, no maneuvers; a placeholder until the payload is in a TLE feed) and then returns to the pad marker, and a `launch-<id>-payload` satellite entity follows the same orbit. Launches reported as failed skip the orbit and return to the pad marker after the ascent. Real flights differ by tens of seconds and a few percent. Everything in the UI is labelled MODELED until real rows arrive.

**Vehicles.** `VEHICLES` in `packages/core/src/ascent-model.ts` lists the vehicle profiles; `vehicleProfileFor` picks one from the LL2 vehicle and launch name. Falcon 9 is the reference (and the only one with a booster return); Electron uses the same ascent shape with its own rough timeline (MECO about T+2:30 at 78 km, SECO about T+8:55 into 190 x 520 km, figures from public webcast timelines and unverified, flagged as such in the Launch tab's assumptions). Vehicles that are not listed (Falcon Heavy, Ariane 6, Atlas V, Vulcan, Starship, Long March, ...) stay at the pad: their ascents (side boosters, long core burns, Starship's suborbital arc) look nothing like this shape and a profile would be misleading. To add a vehicle with a Falcon-9-like two-stage ascent, add a `VehicleProfile` (name regex, `recoverable`, `timeline`, assumption text) to `VEHICLES` and `VEHICLE_LIST`; the model, the chart, the events and the entity pick it up.

**Where launch trajectories can come from** (what each source gives and what the app does with it):

| Source | What it provides | Status here |
|---|---|---|
| Measured webcast overlays (SpaceX, Rocket Lab and others show T+, altitude, speed) | the only real ascent telemetry | typed or scripted into `POST /api/telemetry/:launchId`; reading the webcast automatically (OCR) is a stub |
| Flight Club (flightclub.io) simulations | community-simulated profiles for many vehicles, no stable public API | export by hand into `data/trajectories/` (see Trajectory files) |
| Launch Library 2 | pad, window, target orbit, recovery plan; no trajectories | used for the pad, azimuth, inclination and booster plan |
| Operator press kits and mission pages | typical event times (MECO, fairing, SECO) | the numbers behind the vehicle profiles; entered by hand |
| Space-Track / CelesTrak | the payload's real orbit once it is catalogued, hours after launch | used for the satellites, not for the ascent |
| FAA and NGA hazard areas (NOTAMs) | keep-out areas along the ground track | not implemented (the polygon parser is on hold) |

**Modeled booster return.** A recovered first stage also gets its own entity, `launch-<id>-booster` (kind `stage`, `parentId` the launch, the rocket-stage model), and a `booster` track in `GET /api/telemetry/:launchId` (`modeled.booster`). It shares the vehicle's ascent up to stage separation and then follows a kinematic reconstruction in the launch plane (`packages/core/src/booster.ts`): a ballistic coast (apogee about 125 km), a boostback burn (RTLS) or a short trim (droneship) that sets the horizontal speed, a coast to the entry burn, a cubic descent to the landing-burn start (0.3 km/s, 3.75 km) and a constant-deceleration landing burn. The horizontal speed between the entry burn and the landing burn is not a chosen curve: drag and thrust act along the velocity, so it falls as exp(-integral of k) with k = rho(z)·|v|/(2·beta) plus T/(m·|v|) during the entry burn (beta = m/(Cd·A), the standard-atmosphere density `airDensity`, and the vehicle's `booster` physics: mass, drag area, Cd, entry-burn thrust and length, `FALCON9_BOOSTER` for Falcon 9). With the rough Falcon 9 figures about 5 to 8 percent of the held horizontal speed is left at the landing burn, and the terminal velocity at its altitude (0.21 km/s) is close to the assumed 0.3 km/s. Mass, area and Cd are rough, so treat the shape as plausible rather than accurate. The held horizontal speed is solved so the touchdown lands on the target: 8 km from the pad for RTLS and 600 km for a droneship, both along the launch azimuth (LL2 gives only the landing location's abbreviation, not coordinates, so the droneship's real position is not used). The booster events (`BOOSTBACK`, `ENTRY_BURN`, `LANDING_BURN`, `LANDING`) now carry an altitude and speed from this track. Timing constants live in `BOOSTER_SHAPE`; nothing about the path is measured. There is no booster entity when a trajectory file supplies the vehicle path.

**Ingest.** Send measured rows (for example from a webcast OCR script or typed by hand):

```
curl -X POST http://localhost:8787/api/telemetry/<LL2 launch id> -H "content-type: application/json" -d '[
  {"tSec": 0,  "altKm": 0,    "speedKmS": 0},
  {"tSec": 60, "altKm": 8.4,  "speedKmS": 0.38},
  {"tSec": 120,"altKm": 38.0, "speedKmS": 1.25}
]'
```

`tSec` is seconds since liftoff (T+0 is the launch NET), `altKm` kilometres, `speedKmS` the Earth-relative speed in km/s (convert km/h by dividing by 3600). Rows are validated (finite numbers, `tSec` 0..7200, `altKm` -0.5..2000, `speedKmS` 0..15, at most 2000 rows per request, 7200 stored) and merged by `tSec`, so a script can post a row or a batch every second; add `"replace": true` to start over. The object form `{"rows": [...], "events": [{"name": "MECO", "tSec": 151}], "azimuthDeg": 158, "source": "webcast OCR", "replace": false}` also reports events (names `A-Z0-9_`, e.g. `MECO`, `SECO`, `STAGE_SEP`, `LANDING`; they replace the modeled time of the same name) and an azimuth (default: the modeled one). Downrange is integrated from the rows; the path is measured up to the last row and then follows the modeled shape re-anchored at the last measured point (reported as `liveExtrapolatedFromSec` and a lower sample `confidence`), or holds the last fix when the launch has no model. The call answers with `{ok, source, rows, lastTSec, extrapolatedFromSec}` and the server pushes `entities.changed`. `DELETE /api/telemetry/:launchId` clears the series. Posting and deleting are accepted from localhost sockets only (and only without a foreign `Origin`); rows are kept in memory and in the SQLite cache (7 days) so they survive a restart. `GET /api/telemetry/:launchId` returns `{launchId, entityId, liftoffMs, source, modeled, file, live}` (the modeled series with events and phases next to the posted rows).

**UI.** The Launch tab ends with an ASCENT TELEMETRY section: altitude, speed and downrange charts against T+ (modeled dashed once measured rows exist, measured solid), a cursor that follows the simulation clock (scrub, pause, rate), event markers (MAX-Q, MECO, SEP, SES, FAIRING, SECO and the booster ENTRY, LAND BURN, LANDING) with the current phase highlighted, readouts at the cursor, an events table and a data table. Before liftoff it shows the countdown and the nominal MAX-Q, MECO and SECO values. On the globe the same path is drawn by the usual path overlay with phase labels (stage 1 ascent, staging, stage 2 burn, orbit (held), on orbit (modeled)). Try it offline with `?mock=1` (a Starlink Group 15-15 launch from Vandenberg about 40 minutes ahead): scrub to its NET plus 4 minutes. The mock's Starlink Group 10-31 launch flew 3 hours ago: select it (or its `(payload)` satellite, listed under Earth) to see the modeled orbit; scrub a launch past T+1110 s and the board flips to ON ORBIT while the vehicle carries on along the orbit instead of returning to the pad, which it does only when the 12 hours of the modeled orbit are over (a late jump to the pad marker; the payload then has no position).

**Limits.** There is no OCR here (the `webcast-ocr` connector stays a stub). The model knows nothing about the actual payload mass, trajectory shaping or weather, its inclination for a given mission can be wrong when the provider data does not state it, and non-Falcon vehicles have no modeled ascent (they stay at the pad unless a trajectory file or posted rows exist). A launch id that LL2 no longer lists (older than the previous-10 window) cannot receive rows.

## Architecture

```
connectors -> Hub (EntityGraph + launches/news/dsn/iss state) -> REST + WebSocket -> web store -> scene / panels
     |
  SQLite kv cache (TTL, stale fallback)
```

- **Server** (`apps/server/src`): Fastify with `@fastify/compress`, `@fastify/cors` and `@fastify/websocket`. `main.ts` builds the connector list and the `Scheduler` (`connector.ts`): jittered starts, per-connector timers, exponential backoff, topic gating, health records. `http.ts` is the fetch layer (`cachedFetch`: fresh cache hit, else network, else stale cache); it and the other network users call `net/guarded-fetch.ts`, the only place that touches the network, which asks the persistent limiter (`net/limiter.ts`, [Rate limits](#rate-limits)) first. `store.ts` is the SQLite key/value cache (entries expired for more than 30 days are pruned at startup). `hub.ts` holds shared state and debounces `entities.changed` (250 ms).
- **Core** (`packages/core/src`): body-agnostic. Bodies (`SUN`, `EARTH`, `MOON`, `MARS`) and frames `<BODY>:inertial|fixed`, state providers (`sgp4`, `sampled`, `keplerian`, `geodetic`, `relative`, `composite`), the `EntityGraph` that resolves parents and converts frames, the mission state machine (`deriveMissionState`), the simulation `Clock`, and ascent helpers. Units are km, km/s and ms since epoch.
- **Web** (`apps/web/src`): React 18 + zustand store (`state/store.ts`). The raw three.js `SceneController` (`scene/`) is not wrapped in r3f. SGP4 entities propagate in `workers/propagator.worker.ts` into a transferable `Float32Array`; other providers are evaluated on the main thread through an `EntityGraph` (`state/runtime.ts`). `api/client.ts` chooses the REST client or the mock API (`mock/`).
- **Models**: `scene/models/registry.ts` maps `render.model` keys to `ModelDef`s (true-size bounds, LOD distance, minimum follow distance, fit distance, attitude mode, builder). Builders (`iss.ts`, `satellite.ts`, `rocket-stage.ts`) use the primitive kit in `kit.ts` (shared dimmable materials, named joints, a canvas-drawn solar cell texture) in metres in a body frame (+X forward, +Y starboard, +Z nadir). `ModelLayer` owns the instances: each frame, for the few model entities, it takes the cheap point-buffer position, rejects anything beyond the LOD distance, else resolves the exact state, subtracts the camera in float64, applies the LOD rules (`lib/model-lod.ts`), orients the model (`lib/attitude.ts`) and poses its joints (`lib/arrays.ts` for the ISS). `SceneController` only feeds it positions, visibility, scope and joint callbacks and hides the replaced sprites.
- **Frame of reference**: store `frame` (`Centre`: body or entity), `frameLocked`, `setFrame(centre, {lock?, fly?})`, `setFrameLocked`, `useFrameInfo()` (label, breadcrumb crumbs). `lib/frame.ts` holds the pure maths (`centrePositionEci`, `relativeState`, `framePathPoint` = r(t_i) - c(t_i) + c(ref), labels). `samplePath` and `EntityOverlay` take a `FrameSampler`; the buffers are built around the centre at build time and `RelPositions.shift` translates them by c(now) - ref each frame, so they follow a moving centre without rebuilds (rebuilt when the centre or the window changes). `SceneController.setFrame` mirrors the store; `focus({centre})` pivots the camera (a body through the body view, an entity through the follow machinery).
- **Paths and frames**: `lib/paths.ts` samples `provider.stateAt` over a window (<= ~480 points plus exact composite phase boundaries, buffers reused, rebuilt only when the window has slid a tenth of its length). Everything is drawn in the Earth-inertial scene, with one exception: when every sample is natively `EARTH:fixed` and the window is <= 45 min (the booster, launch ascents), or the plan is a whole planned launch flight (anchored) whose keplerian orbit tail is converted to `EARTH:fixed` sample by sample, the path stays in Earth-fixed coordinates and is rotated by the Earth's spin each frame, so a booster standing on its landing pad does not draw a 460 m/s eastward streak; the marker, evaluated at the current time, still sits on it. Inertial points are drawn at their true position. Path buffers are float64.
- **Floating origin (precision)**: all positions are kept in float64 on the CPU (the SGP4 worker also returns float64) and the camera position is subtracted there; the GPU only ever sees camera-relative coordinates (`scene/relative.ts`, `lib/floating-origin.ts`). The render camera sits at the origin, the Earth and atmosphere objects are moved to minus the camera position. This is what keeps objects and trajectories steady when the camera is hundreds of metres from them (a float32 holds 0.5 m at Earth radius). Everything in one frame, including the followed anchor, is evaluated at one simulation time.
- **Draw order and depth**: the renderer uses a logarithmic depth buffer (depth precision is uniform in log distance, near 5 m, far 1e13 km). Earth, atmosphere, paths and orbit lines are depth tested. Marker sprites (satellites, probes, pads, selection ring) are not: a sprite has a single depth for all its pixels and was half cut by the Earth at the limb. Their vertex shader instead hides a marker whose line of sight crosses the Earth ellipsoid or the displayed Moon or Sun sphere (`lib/occlusion.ts`, same maths on the CPU for picking, the RENDERED count and path labels; the spheres are the drawn ones), so markers never clip into the ground at any rotation or zoom and ground markers need no lift (paths and markers are nudged 5 m up). Stars write depth 1.0, so the opaque Earth and everything in front of the far plane occludes them.

## Extending

- **New data feed**: add a file in `apps/server/src/connectors/` exporting a `Connector` (`id`, optional `intervalMs`, `startDelayMs`, `topic`, `network: false`, `start`, `run`, `stop`, or `stub`). Use `ctx.cachedFetch(key, url, ttlMs, parse)` for network access and `ctx.hub.upsert(...)` / `hub.setLaunches` / `hub.setDsn` etc. to publish. Add it to the `CONNECTORS` array in `apps/server/src/main.ts`. Throw from `run` to report an error and back off; call `ctx.softFail(msg, retryMs)` for a partial failure.
- **New entity kind** (rover, drone, lander): emit an `EntityRecord` with any `kind` string and a provider spec; no core change is needed. Optional `render` hints set `glyph` (`dot|diamond|ring|triangle|square`), `colorKey`, `trailSeconds`, `footprint`, `orbitLine` and `label`. The colour comes from the entity's category (`categoryOf` in `apps/web/src/lib/browser-ui.ts`: the kind decides, `colorKey` is only a hint), so a new kind gets a colour by being mapped to one of the nine categories there; a new category needs a `CATEGORIES` entry (with its colour), a `POINT_SIZE` entry in `apps/web/src/scene/palette.ts` and a `--c-<category>` variable in `apps/web/src/styles.css` (`palette.test.ts` checks the three agree and that the colours stay distinguishable).
- **Entity that changes phase** (a landing booster): use a `composite` provider chaining `sampled` ascent, `relative` landing arc and `geodetic` landed segments. The launch ascent composites (`packages/core/src/ascent-model.ts`) and the booster composites in `apps/server/src/connectors/launchlibrary.ts` are complete examples.
- **New 3D model**: write a builder returning a `ModelInstance` (use `ModelKit` in `apps/web/src/scene/models/kit.ts`: metres, +X forward, +Y starboard, +Z nadir, optional named joints) and register its `ModelDef` in `scene/models/registry.ts`; then set `render.model` to its key on any entity. Entities need a provider with a state (a velocity gives the orientation); unknown keys are ignored.
- **New body**: its appearance (mean colour, bands for a giant, albedo noise, `rings: {innerKm, outerKm}` for a ring system) is an entry in `apps/web/src/lib/body-style.ts`, keyed by the registry id (grey without one). Define a `Body` (radius, flattening, GM, rotation, approximate orbit around its parent) in `packages/core/src/bodies.ts` and add it to `DEFAULT_BODY_LIST` (or call `defaultBodies.register`); for a real data stream add a target in `connectors/horizons-bodies.ts` (`buildBodyTargets`). Frames `<BODY>:inertial|fixed` and conversions then work for it, and `/api/bodies` lists it. The entity tree's body nodes and the breadcrumb come from the body entities, so the new body shows up there without UI work (the scene still needs to draw it).
- **New state provider**: add its spec to the `ProviderSpec` union in `packages/core/src/types.ts`, implement `StateProvider` (`stateAt`, `toSpec`) in `packages/core/src/providers/`, then add a `case` in `providerFromSpec` and an export in `providers/index.ts`. Server and web both rebuild providers through that function.

## Layout

- **Resizing**: drag the borders between the entity list and the globe, the globe and the detail panel, and the globe and the bottom row. Double-click a border (or press Enter on it) to reset it to its default. Borders are keyboard focusable: arrow keys move by 16 px (Shift: 64 px), Home/End jump to the minimum/maximum. Sizes are clamped (the globe always keeps at least 360 x 200 px) and re-clamped when the window shrinks.
- **Optional panels**: the View menu in the top bar (Panels section) shows and hides Launches and the News ticker, see [Controls](#controls). The grid reclaims the space (`--ticker-h` and `--bottom-h` become 0), the splitters that no longer have two neighbours are not rendered, and the sizes you set stay stored, so a panel comes back at its previous size. State and pure parse/serialise logic: `state/panels.ts` (`usePanels`, `setPanel('board', true)` opens a panel from code); menu: `ui/ViewMenu.tsx`, state `state/view.ts`.
- **Persistence**: sizes are stored in `localStorage` under `eio.layout.v1` (per browser; a default size is simply not stored). If storage is unavailable the layout works but is not remembered. At 820 px and narrower the page stacks vertically and the borders are hidden.
- **Implementation**: sizes are CSS variables (`--left-w`, `--right-w`, `--bottom-h`) on `.app` set by `ui/ResizableLayout.tsx`; `ui/Splitter.tsx` is the reusable pointer-capture splitter; pure clamp and persistence logic is in `lib/layout.ts`. The three.js canvas follows through the `ResizeObserver` in `SceneController`, which resizes the drawing buffer and redraws synchronously (before the browser paints), so dragging a border does not flash a blank globe; the canvas CSS size is 100% of its container.
- **Displayed values**: all number formatting goes through `lib/format.ts` with fixed decimals per unit (lat/lon 3, altitude 1, distances by unit band (km, Mkm, AU), speed 3 km/s or 0 km/h, period in minutes 1, light time 1 s, data rates scaled to b/s, kb/s, Mb/s, Gb/s). Server `attrs` are formatted by key (`rangeKm`, `rtltSec`, `dataRateBps`, ...) and any other long decimal inside attribute or telemetry text is rounded to 3 decimals. Numeric cells use tabular digits and stay on one line (ellipsis when too narrow); only ids and URLs may break anywhere.

## CelesTrak blocked or unreachable

- **Symptom**: the `celestrak` connector reports `fetch failed (UND_ERR_CONNECT_TIMEOUT)` (also `ECONNRESET`, `ETIMEDOUT`), only `stations` or nothing loads, and the health note says `skipped N groups (...)`. That is a TCP connect timeout to `celestrak.org:443`, not an HTTP 403: other hosts work, and the app cannot see a rate limit at that level. It was seen while CelesTrak itself was down (confirmed server-side on 2026-10-24); an IP-level block or an egress problem looks identical from here, so the probe below is the first thing to run. A connect error stops the run at the first failure instead of trying all 11 groups and keeps the short retry schedule (30 min, doubling up to 12 h), because an outage usually ends on its own; HTTP 403/429 wait 3 h first and `Retry-After` is honoured. Downloads are spaced 1 to 2 s apart.
- **Probe**: `npm run celestrak:probe` (or `npm run celestrak:probe -- gps-ops` for another group) makes exactly one request with the server's headers and a 20 s timeout and prints the status, the response headers (`server`, `cf-ray`, `retry-after`), the first 300 body characters, or the error with its cause code. A connect timeout there confirms the network problem; a 403 with a body is a real rate limit.
- **What to try**: another network or a phone hotspot; save the group JSON from a browser on a network that works (`https://celestrak.org/NORAD/elements/gp.php?GROUP=starlink&FORMAT=json`) as `data/celestrak/<group>.json`, which the connector reads whenever nothing is cached; or wait for the stored retry time (shown in the health note as `waiting until HH:MMZ`). Stale cache entries are served meanwhile, and a cached group is re-requested with `If-Modified-Since`, so a `304` costs no download.
- **The earlier 403 text is gone**: each failure overwrites the message in that group's backoff row (`backoff:celestrak:<group>` in `data/cache.sqlite`), so a 403 seen earlier is replaced by whatever failed last, usually the connect timeout. The probe is the way to see what CelesTrak answers now.

## Known issues

- **CelesTrak 403**: CelesTrak answers HTTP 403 to repeated downloads of unchanged groups. The server caches each group for 3 h and, after a 403, leaves that group alone for 3 h (doubling to 12 h while it keeps failing, persisted across restarts; earlier versions retried every 30 min, which can keep a block alive). On a cold start with a blocked IP `active` and/or `starlink` may therefore be missing and only stations load. Ways out: wait; set `SPACETRACK_USER`/`SPACETRACK_PASSWORD` to get Starlink from Space-Track; or save the group's JSON from a browser (`https://celestrak.org/NORAD/elements/gp.php?GROUP=starlink&FORMAT=json`) as `data/celestrak/starlink.json`, which is used when nothing is cached. The connector reports `partial: ...` in its health note. The debris groups (`cosmos-2251-debris`, `iridium-33-debris`, `fengyun-1c-debris`) are fetched after the main three and were only tested against recorded fixtures: while CelesTrak blocks this machine they are missing, so the launch filter then has no live debris groups (it still groups any objects that share a COSPAR launch id, e.g. Starlink batches). `cosmos-1408-debris` is not offered because CelesTrak's index page does not list that group.
- **Launch groups**: derived only from the COSPAR designator, so objects without one (launches, probes, ground stations) are never in a group, and launch groups are not linked to Launch Library 2 `launch-<id>` entities (LL2 carries no COSPAR id). A group is labelled by its `A` piece, else the most common name; Starlink batches therefore show the name of one satellite. The scene fit on selection needs a handler on `runtime.onFitRequest`; until one is installed the camera does not move.
- **Horizons ephemerides**: Horizons has no ephemeris for M20 (Mars 2020) and MAVEN, so those probes have no position. The connector logs them as failed targets and retries hourly.
- **ISS telemetry items**: the item ids in the reference document were wrong (`USLAB000032/33` are not position and velocity as a pair, `S0000001`-`S0000004` are not four array angles); the table in `iss-live.ts` now follows the ISS Mimic project's list and only the items marked verified in [ISS array telemetry](#iss-array-telemetry) are claimed to be what they say. The BGA angles are shown as the feed reports them and never drive the 3D model. The wheretheiss.at fix is still the source for the live-fix marker.
- **3D models**: the models are procedural approximations built from public dimensions (no CAD): module placement, the radiators and the arm are approximate, the ISS carries no docked vehicles besides one Progress, and there is no self-shadowing or Earth-lit shading beyond one earthshine light. The ISS attitude is the measured one only when live arrays are fresh and the clock is live; otherwise (scrubbed time, mock mode, offline) the model flies the plain LVLH attitude, which differs from the real one by several degrees. The point-to-model switch is decided per entity from the camera distance, so a model is never drawn for an entity the scope policy has removed.
- **`npm audit`**: reports advisories in dev tooling only (Vite 5, Vitest 2). Upgrading means major versions and is deferred.
- **Stub connectors**: Space-Track, SatNOGS, NOTAM and webcast OCR do nothing yet.
- **Mars view**: Mars is a sphere of its equatorial radius (3396.19 km; the 1/169.8 flattening is ignored, as the USGS map is a sphere-based simple cylindrical map) with the Viking MDIM 2.1 colour map (`/textures/mars.jpg`, `npm run assets`; scaled down to the GPU's texture limit), lit from the Sun and oriented each frame by its IAU rotation (pole RA 317.68 deg, Dec 52.89 deg, 350.892 deg per day) with the pole on the mesh's z axis and longitude 0 at the map centre, so a polar orbit crosses the map's poles and objects on `MARS:fixed` sit over the right surface features. Without the file Mars is a plain tinted sphere. Its position is the approximate Kepler orbit, and the rotation is the IAU linear model (no precession or periodic terms). Entities on other bodies (a geodetic provider on `MARS:fixed`, say; none ship by default) are positioned through the approximate body ephemeris and drawn as points on the Earth-centred scene. The Moon is placed with a low-precision almanac series (about 0.3 degrees, 0.5 percent in distance) and the Sun likewise; the Moon is drawn with the IAU mean rotation, so libration is not shown. Trails and paths are sampled in the active frame of reference (see Features), so an Earth satellite's trail seen from the Moon or Sun frame is the true, open relative curve (its orbit line stays the ellipse round the Earth). The Moon and the Sun, at true size, are a fraction of a pixel when the whole Earth-Moon system is in view; they are clickable with a 14 px minimum hit area (but an object under the cursor wins over a body, so LRO hides the Moon from far out), and the tree and the breadcrumb find them too.

## Known limitations / roadmap

Implemented from `plans.md`: the two UI/UX requests (stable value layout, resizable panels), the launch filter, the camera controls, the Earth occluding the stars and trajectories for non-SGP4 objects. Performance was reported fine, including with `?mock=1`. Also implemented: not rendering objects outside the focus object's scope, true-scale distances for probes and bodies, and the precision/clipping/resize-flicker fixes. The scene has no terrain and the downloaded Earth map is 8192 px (4.9 km per texel), so the closest views (3 km up) depend on the streamed DETAIL tiles (0.6 km per pixel at best) and are blurry beyond that. Paths show the provider's own samples: a probe whose Horizons data is missing (M20, MAVEN) has none, and Voyager-class paths are sub-pixel from far out (they are 4 million km long over 3 days, visible when zoomed in). Mars orbiters fetched from Horizons are geocentric (30 min steps), too coarse for a 2 h orbit: they would need a Mars-centred fetch like LRO's.
