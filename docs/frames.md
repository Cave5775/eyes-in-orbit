# Frame of reference: design and contract

Status: design for the plans.md item "Restructure the scene to change the frame of reference to the selected celestial body in a hierarchical system". Written before the implementation; the implementing agents follow it and update it when they learn something that changes it.

## Current model: one focus, one selection action

This section is the contract as implemented now; the sections below are the original design and the notes of the phases that built it, and where they disagree this one wins (they are corrected in place where they would mislead).

**Bodies are entities.** Everywhere outside `packages/core` and `lib/ref-body.ts` a body is its entity (`body-earth`, `body-moon`, ...). The registry id (`EARTH`) exists in core (`BodyRegistry`, `Body`, `provider.bodyId`, `EntityRecord.bodyId` = the primary body an object orbits) and in `ref-body.ts` (radius, view distances). There is ONE converter, core `bodyOf(rec)` (the registry id a body entity stands for, else undefined), and ONE predicate, core `isOrbitable(rec)` (a body entity, or an entity with `attrs.orbitable === true`). `orbitableBodyId`, `bodyCentre`, `entityCentre`, `isOrbitableBody`, `isRefBody`, the scene's `bodyTarget` / `bodyRegId` / `bodyOfEntity` / `focusBody` and the top-bar body tabs are gone.

**The focus is four fields** in the store: `selectedId`, `frame` (an entity id, default `body-earth`; `Centre` is that string, `EARTH_CENTRE`, with `isEarthCentre` as the identity fast path: the Earth is the origin, every output equals the pre-frame behaviour), `frameLocked` and `follow`. `focus` is only the one-shot camera request that goes with a change (`{id, nonce}`, `{lat, lon, nonce}` for a pad, `{nonce}` for the home view).

**One selection action**, `selectObject(id, {fly = true})`; every route calls it: tree body nodes and rows, list rows and search results (`EntityBrowser`), breadcrumb crumbs (`FrameBar`), scene clicks and the choice popup (`SceneView`), the DSN panel, launch cards (`focusLaunch`), and the restored frame at load. It
1. selects the entity (the detail panel shows it; an id that is not in the catalog is ignored),
2. sets the frame to it when it is orbitable and the frame is not locked (a locked frame stays; a non-orbitable entity leaves the frame as it is),
3. turns follow on and emits ONE focus request for it (`fly: false` skips both: select and frame without moving the camera).
`selectObject(null)` is a deselect: nothing selected, follow off, the frame back to the Earth unless locked, no camera request. `resetView()` (Esc) is the home view: deselect plus unlock plus a home camera request. `setFrame(id, lock?)` only sets the frame (and the lock): it is what the detail panel's Lock frame key uses for the selected object, and it never selects or moves the camera. A full catalog that no longer has the selected entity clears the selection (and one that lacks the frame's entity sends the frame to the Earth).

**One camera subject.** The scene's camera target is the focus entity: `focus({id})` sets `followId`, the `followFn` (`worldPos(id)`) and `follow`, a body like any other entity (a body gets the whole-body view distance `refViewDistKm`, its own min and max distances, and leaves the surface view; the Earth is followed at the origin, its minimum distance is the usual altitude floor). What the camera follows is read from `follow` and `followId` (`followedRec()`, `followedBody()`), never from a second field, so it cannot go stale: deselecting, Follow off, Fit and a pan all just clear `follow`. `surfaceAvailable()` reads the focus entity (the surface view is off while a non-Earth body is followed) and the frame (a non-Earth body as the frame), not a remembered body.

**Orbit lines** (see "Orbit-line rule"): the selected entity's orbit at full strength and its children's dimmed; the frame and the camera do not change which lines exist.

**Persistence and migration.** `localStorage['eio.frame']` is `{"kind":"entity","id":"body-moon"}`. The previous format `{"kind":"body","id":"MARS"}` (registry id) still loads and becomes `body-mars`. The shape written is the one the previous build read for an entity frame, so an older build still understands it. At the first full catalog the store restores the frame unlocked and selects it (the camera flies there; `SceneView` replays a request that arrived before the scene existed); unknown or stale ids fall back to the Earth.

## What the user saw originally, and why it was wrong (the design that started this)

The (then) EARTH / MOON / SUN tabs only moved the camera. With the Moon selected and the Lunar Reconnaissance Orbiter (LRO) picked, the LRO's path is a wavy line relative to the Earth, not a closed orbit around the Moon, because
1. LRO is a `probe` with `bodyId: 'EARTH'` and geocentric Horizons samples (`apps/server/src/connectors/horizons.ts`, `CENTER '500@399'`, `frame: 'EARTH:inertial'`, 30 min steps), and
2. paths, trails and orbit lines are sampled in `EARTH:inertial` and only *translated* with the Earth (`lib/paths.ts`, `scene/overlays.ts`), never re-expressed relative to the chosen body at each sample time.

## Model

**Body tree.** `packages/core/src/bodies.ts` already has SUN -> EARTH -> MOON and SUN -> MARS (`parentId`, `lineage()`), and `convert` / `hopInertial` / `EntityGraph.stateIn(id, t, '<BODY>:inertial')` can express any state in any body's inertial frame (all inertial frames share the equatorial axes, so hops are translations). Every entity has a primary body, `EntityRecord.bodyId` (the body it orbits or stands on): satellites EARTH, LRO MOON, deep-space probes stay as they are.

**Centre.** The frame of reference is a centre, an entity (a body entity or any other):
```ts
type Centre = string;   // an entity id, default 'body-earth' (the original design had {kind: 'body'|'entity', id})
```
Frames are translations only (no rotation): a centre at position `c(t)` in Earth-inertial coordinates. Entities stay stored and rendered in Earth-inertial coordinates; the renderer is camera-relative, so *where things are drawn now* does not depend on the frame. What the frame changes is everything that depends on time:
- **paths and trails** are sampled in the frame: a sample at time `t_i` is `r(t_i) - c(t_i)` (position relative to the centre at that time) drawn around the centre's *current* position, `+ c(now)`. An Earth satellite's trail and path seen from a Moon frame are the true relative curve. An orbit LINE is not sampled in the frame: it stays the ellipse round the entity's primary body (see "Orbit-line rule"). With the centre Earth this must reproduce today's output exactly (regression guard).
- **readouts** (range, speed) are relative to the centre: subtraction of two `stateIn(..., 'EARTH:inertial')` (body entities are in the main-thread graph, the Earth is the origin).
- **the camera** goes to whatever is selected; selecting an orbitable entity also makes it the frame, a locked frame stays where it is.

**Selection vs frame vs lock** (the store, `apps/web/src/state/store.ts`):
```ts
frame: Centre;            // an entity id
frameLocked: boolean;     // locked: selecting objects never changes the frame
selectObject(id: string | null, opts?: { fly?: boolean }): void;   // THE selection action, see "Current model"
setFrame(id: Centre, lock?: boolean): void;                         // frame (and lock) only
setFrameLocked(locked: boolean): void;
```
- `selectObject(id)` selects, follows and flies; when not locked and the entity is *orbitable* (a body entity, or an entity flagged `attrs.orbitable`) the frame becomes it.
- "Lock the frame to the object": the detail panel's button is `setFrame(selectedId, true)`; then other objects can be selected and their paths and readouts are relative to it.
- `resetView()` sets the frame to Earth and unlocks.
- Surface view stays Earth-only.

**Primary-body physics.** Orbit period and altitude use the primary body's `gm` and radius (`apps/web/src/lib/orbit.ts` is hard-wired to Earth; `computeTelemetry` already uses `rec.bodyId` for geodetic coordinates).

**Data.** LRO (Horizons id -85) must be fetched Moon-centred: `CENTER '500@301'`, `frame: 'MOON:inertial'`, `bodyId: 'MOON'`, a step fine enough for a ~2 h orbit (about 5 min, with Hermite interpolation from the velocities Horizons returns) over a window that stays modest (the per-target window/step must be explicit in `dsn-targets.ts`/`horizons.ts`). The drawn Moon comes from the low-precision series (`moonPositionEci`, about 0.3 degrees, up to ~2000 km); LRO is expressed relative to that same Moon by the frame hop, so it stays on the drawn Moon. Horizons vectors are ICRF J2000 and the core frames are equatorial of date (about 0.36 degrees of precession in 2026): a tilt of ~11 km at lunar distance, accepted and documented, not fixed here.

**UI** (later phase): a breadcrumb `SUN > EARTH > MOON` with a lock toggle shows and sets the frame; the entity browser groups entities under their primary body as a tree; a Lock action in the selection; the DSN panel moves into the View menu (plans.md UI/UX item).

## Implementation notes (FRAME, phase 1)

Where the implementation decided something or differs from the text above:
- **Store API**: `frame`, `frameLocked`, `selectObject`, `setFrame(id, lock?)`, `setFrameLocked(locked)` (see "Current model"). Selectors `selectFrame`, `selectFrameLocked`, `frameInfoOf(state)` and the hook `useFrameInfo()` return `{centre, locked, isDefault, label, crumbs, chain}`. `crumbs` is the breadcrumb root first (`Sun > Earth > Moon`, the entity last for a non-body centre), one crumb per entity carrying its `id` (what a click selects); the labels are the entities' names, and the body entities generated from the registry name the Earth's crumb before the catalog has arrived.
- `setFrame`: an explicit call always applies, locked or not (the lock only blocks `selectObject`'s frame change). `lock` omitted leaves the lock as is. It never selects and never moves the camera.
- `selectObject(id)`: sets the frame when not locked and the entity is orbitable (`isOrbitable`: a body entity, or `attrs.orbitable === true`, e.g. a large asteroid: its frame is an entity frame through the existing entity-centre code, with no camera policy of its own).
- **Paths, trails and body orbit lines in a frame.** (The orbit line of an object is always in its primary body's frame, never the active frame.) A sample is `r(t_i) - c(t_i) + c(ref)` with `ref` the build time. The buffers are not rebuilt as the centre moves: `RelPositions.shift` translates them by `c(now) - c(ref)` on upload, so they follow a moving centre (the Moon moves ~26,000 km between rebuilds of a 3-day path). Positions are never moved (everything is drawn to scale). Rebuilt when the centre or window changes.
- The entity that is the centre is drawn relative to its **primary body** (`frameForRec`): relative to itself it would be a point.
- A path or orbit is hidden while the centre entity has no state at the build time (outside its valid range).
- Footprints and the ground track are Earth-surface features of Earth-bound objects (`rec.bodyId === 'EARTH'`) and ignore the frame; LRO gets neither.
- **Orbit period** (`lib/orbit.ts`): gm and radius of `rec.bodyId`; closed-orbit limit 5e5 km for the Earth (as before), 1e5 km otherwise; the 100 km atmosphere margin is Earth-only (LRO's periapsis is ~30 km above the Moon). `orbitPeriodSec(graph, rec, s, tMs?)` converts `s` to the primary's inertial frame when `tMs` is given.
- **Readouts**: `computeTelemetry(graph, rec, t, centre)` adds `refRangeKm` / `refSpeedKmS` for a centre that is not the Earth (difference of two Earth-inertial states, a body entity included); none for the entity itself or an unknown centre. The centre entity must be in the `EntityGraph` (`ensureInGraph`; the scene does it for the frame centre).
- HUD: `Camera d from <Label>` is now the frame, not the camera's anchor, with ` (locked)` appended when locked. `d` is the camera's distance to the frame centre (`distanceFromCentre`, `SceneController.hudDistanceKm`), not to the camera's target: with a locked frame the camera follows the selection, so the two differ. Without a frame label (default frame, unlocked) it stays the distance to the target.
- Not done in phase 1 (breadcrumb, lock toggle, Lock action and entity tree are now in the UI section below; the View menu is the MENU agent's). (Mars was not yet a frame centre then; it is one now, like any body entity.) The frame was not persisted then; it is now (see "Current model").

## UI (phase 2, what exists)

- **Frame bar** (`ui/FrameBar.tsx`, mounted at the top left of the scene in `SceneView`): `FRAME  SUN > EARTH > MOON > LRO` from `useFrameInfo().crumbs`; the logic of the chain stays in `lib/frame.ts` (`frameCrumbs`). A crumb click is `selectObject(crumb.id)`; a crumb whose entity is not in the catalog is disabled (Mars is a normal crumb). The lock toggle calls `setFrameLocked`; a locked frame is amber (`Locked`, `[LOCKED]` in the HUD too). A hint line (`NO STATE FOR <X> AT THIS TIME: PATHS HIDDEN`) appears when the centre is an entity that has no state at the sim time; it is a 500 ms poll of the main-thread graph (`graph.stateIn`), independent of the scene's own path hiding, so it also shows when nothing is selected.
- **Lock matrix** (`test/frame-matrix.test.ts`, `test/frame-matrix-scene.test.ts`, the sequences in `test/frame-store.test.ts`): every kind of mock object (Sun, Earth, Moon, Mars, an SGP4 satellite, Moon and Mars orbiters, deep-space probes, a launch vehicle, booster, payload, a ground station) as the centre at several sim times, including outside a sampled entity's data range. Invariants: the centre is at 0 in its own frame, a path sample is `r(t_i) - c(t_i) + c(ref)`, readouts are `|r - c|`, no NaN. Rules found by it: the centre must be in the main-thread graph (an SGP4 centre is put there by `ensureCentreInGraph`, which now also runs at the end of `setEntities` because that call removes a replaced SGP4 record); a path built while the centre had no position is empty and is rebuilt as soon as the centre has one (an anchored flight is never rebuilt by the clock); an object standing on the ground (ground station, vehicle on the pad: a geodetic provider) has no path in the Earth frame but gets a -3 h .. +3 h one relative to any other centre (`planPathWindow` `framed`, `setFrame` re-evaluates the selected path).
- **Lock action** (`ui/DetailPanel.tsx`): in the row of keys under the name, `Lock frame` = `setFrame(rec.id, true)` (the object is already selected and followed: no camera request); while the selection is the locked frame it reads `Frame locked` = `setFrameLocked(false)` (the frame stays on the object until another body is selected, which would then re-centre again). Any entity can be the frame; nothing restricts it to objects that "reasonably" orbit (a ground station gives a rotating-Earth wobble because frames do not rotate).
- **Entity tree** (`ui/EntityBrowser.tsx`, pure part `lib/entity-tree.ts`): TREE (default, remembered in `localStorage` `eio.browser`) / LIST. `buildTreeRows(items, {bodies, frameId, frameBodyId, openAll, overrides, ...})` (the tree nodes are keyed by registry body id because `EntityRecord.bodyId` is; the caller passes the frame's entity id and the body node its path ends at) groups the already classified (`classify`: what the query leaves drawn, hidden groups removed) and ordered items by `bodyId` (a search hit without one is looked up in the entity table, an unknown body hangs off the root) under the body tree from `defaultBodies`, counts each subtree, and emits only the open nodes as flat rows (`body`, `group`, `entity`), all `ROW_H` tall, so `VirtualList` stays unchanged. Order inside a body: sub-bodies first (MOON appears right under EARTH, not after thousands of satellites), then the objects, grouped by kind (largest group first) when the body holds more than 12, else listed directly. Default open: the nodes on the path to the frame (and the group of an entity frame) and, while the query has an only or text term, everything that has a hit; the user's folds are `overrides` by row key and reset when that part of the query changes (hiding a group does not reset them). With no filter active (`skeleton`) every body of the registry is a node, count 0 allowed (Sun, planets and dwarf planets visible, moons folded under their planet); while filtering, only the paths to hits (and bodies that match) are shown. A body-node click is `selectObject` of the body's entity (it opens the node too); so is an entity click. Cost: one pass over the filtered entities per change, rows only for open nodes (12,000 entities collapsed: a handful of rows).
- The top-bar body tabs (SUN / EARTH / MARS / MOON) are gone: bodies are chosen in the tree, the breadcrumb or the scene. Entities flagged `attrs.orbitable` become the frame when selected; nothing in the catalog is flagged yet.

## Bodies as entities (core)

`packages/core/src/hierarchy.ts` turns the body registry into ordinary entities, so celestial bodies are positional objects with trajectories like satellites.
- **Records**: `bodyEntityRecords(registry = defaultBodies, updatedAt = 0)` gives one `EntityRecord` per registry body (35 by default: `DEFAULT_BODY_LIST`, the original four first): ids `body-sun` (kind `star`), `body-earth`, `body-mars`, `body-jupiter`, ... (`planet`), `body-ceres`, `body-pluto`, `body-eris`, `body-haumea`, `body-makemake` (`dwarf_planet`, `Body.dwarf`), `body-moon`, `body-io`, `body-titan`, `body-charon`, ... (`moon`); `group: 'bodies'`; `aliases.body` = registry id, `aliases.alt` = Sol / Terra / Luna; `render: { orbitLine (not the Sun), label, colorKey: 'body' }`; `attrs`: `radiusKm`, `gm`, `periodDays` (orbital period around the parent from `bodyPeriodSec`, not for the Sun), `approx`. `bodyId` is the registry id of the body it ORBITS (Moon `EARTH`, Earth and Mars `SUN`; the Sun names itself because the field is required) and `parentId` the entity id of that body (`body-earth`, unset for the Sun). The hub accepts the records as they are (it restamps `updatedAt`). `EntityRecord.createdAt?` (ms) is for the hub to set on first insert.
- **Provider**: `{ type: 'body', bodyId }` -> `BodyProvider`, state in `EARTH:inertial` from `bodyStateEci(id, t)`: Earth zero, Sun `sunPositionEci` (velocity by central difference), Moon `moonStateEci`, any other body the hop `convert({ frame: '<ID>:inertial', r: [0,0,0] }, 'EARTH:inertial')`. These are the positions the scene draws today, exactly; confidence 1.
- **Rank** (`entityRank`, smaller = higher up; `compareHierarchy` = rank ascending, then `createdAt ?? updatedAt` descending, then id): body entities by `bodyDepth * 1000 + registry index` (Sun, Earth, Mars, Moon), then every other object by `1e6 + bodyDepth(bodyId) + 1`: objects orbiting the Sun, then those orbiting Earth or Mars (same level, ordered by recency), then those orbiting the Moon; an unknown primary body ranks last. All bodies precede all objects, so a body always comes before anything that orbits it.
- **`bodyOf(rec)`**: the registry id a body entity stands for (`provider.type === 'body'`), else `undefined`; **`isOrbitable(rec)`**: a body entity or `attrs.orbitable === true` (a future large asteroid). `isBodyEntity(rec)` tests the provider type. `bodyPeriodSec(body, registry)` is `2 pi sqrt(a^3 / gm_parent)` (Moon about 27.4 d, Earth 365.3 d, Mars 687 d; the elements are approximate).

## UI for bodies as entities (store and browser)

- **Selecting a body**: `selectObject('body-moon')` selects the entity and sets the frame to it when the frame is unlocked (`frameOnSelect` in `lib/frame-store.ts`); ordinary objects never change the frame, a locked frame never changes. A registry id (`'MOON'`) is not an entity id and is ignored.
- **Persistence**: the centre is saved in `localStorage['eio.frame']` (`{"kind":"entity","id":"body-mars"}`; the old `{"kind":"body","id":"MARS"}` still loads, see "Current model") on every frame change; the lock is not saved. When the first full catalog arrives (`upsertEntities(..., true)`) the store restores it with `resolveFrame` (the entity must exist in the catalog, anything else falls back to the Earth and the stored value is overwritten), unlocked, selecting it (so the camera flies there) unless it is the Earth. A frame the user changed before the catalog arrived wins. `resetView` stores the Earth.
- **Reveal on select** (`revealKeys`, `revealRowIndex`, `scrollTopToReveal` in `lib/entity-tree.ts`): a selection that did not come from a click in the browser itself (`EntityBrowser` remembers the id it just selected) opens the body chain and kind group above the row (additive overrides, like the user's own folds, not persisted), then scrolls the virtualised list to the row once the expansion has reached it (centred when out of view, untouched when fully visible). An entity the query leaves out (or hides) is not listed, so it is not revealed and nothing is cleared. A body entity reveals its own node (only the nodes above it open). The frame's path is open by default; the two never conflict because reveal only adds open keys.
- **Sticky headers**: `stickyAncestors(rows, first)` (`lib/entity-tree.ts`) gives the indices of the ancestors of the first row in view (body nodes, then the kind group); `ui/VirtualList.tsx` draws them with the normal row renderer over the top of the list (so a click does what it does on the node), re-reading `scrollTop` once per animation frame and re-rendering only when the first row in view changes. They take at most half the list height; `scrollTopToReveal(..., pinned)` keeps a revealed row below them. Colours: a dot, a point, a chip and a legend entry all use the entity's category colour (`categoryOf`, `CATEGORIES[].color`).
- **Tree**: a tree body node is a body entity: the chevron folds, the name is `selectObject` of its entity (which frames it, follows it and flies there). Mars is a normal node and a normal crumb.
- **Detail panel**: a body entity shows a BODY section (radius, GM, period around the parent in days, parent, approx; `bodyFacts`) instead of the generic period row; `Lock frame` locks the selected entity itself (a body entity is its own frame).
- **Chips**: the chips are the general categories (`categoryOf`, `lib/browser-ui.ts`); specific groups are search tags (`lib/search-tags.ts`: `group:starlink`, `type:probe`, `launch:2024-123`, `-type:debris`...). `capChips` is no longer used by the browser.
- **Hiding from the tree**: a body node and a kind group header carry an eye that hides `body:<id>` (the body and its sub-bodies' objects, so the node's count and the hidden set agree) or `type:<kind>` (the group's own kind, e.g. `type:launch_vehicle`, which hides only that kind): a `hide` term of the query (`lib/query.ts`, `classify`, never for the selection or the objects related to it). Hidden entities leave the scene and the list and the tree alike, so the counts are what is drawn; a body node whose objects are all hidden keeps its row (crossed eye) while its body entity stands for it. Body entities themselves are never hidden.

## Web libs for bodies

- `lib/ref-body.ts`: `RefBody` is any registry body id (the literals `EARTH | MOON | SUN` keep autocompleting); `refPositionEci` is core `bodyStateEci(...).r`; radius, view / min distances work for Mars. `refDisplay` is the plain position with `k = 1` (the field is a leftover of the removed distance compression and can be dropped from `DrawnBody`, `GroupBody` and the pick functions). View distances: the Earth 24,000 km, any other body 4 radii (the Moon 6,950 km, Mars 13,600 km, the Sun 2.8e6 km), so the disc is clear; the zoom-out limit is `MAX_DIST_KM` = 1e11 km for every view. `NEAR_EARTH_LIMIT_KM` (2e6 km) only classifies an object as deep space (no latitude/altitude/period readout, no orbit line of its own, scope), it never moves anything. `isRegistryBody` is any registry body (`isRefBody` and `REF_BODIES` are gone). `refMinDistKm('EARTH')` is the camera's usual altitude floor above the surface.
- `lib/frame.ts`: `Centre` is an entity id, `EARTH_CENTRE` = `body-earth`; `frameForRec(centre, rec)` samples a body entity's own orbit in its parent body's frame whatever the centre is (Moon around the Earth, Earth and Mars around the Sun's current position; the Sun keeps the centre).
- `lib/orbit.ts`: a body entity's period is `bodyPeriodSec` (the Sun: null), not the satellite closed-orbit test.
- `lib/pick.ts`: candidates sort by hierarchy rank (`rankMap` / `PickSource.rank` / `PickExtra.rank` / `PickOptions.ranks`; missing ranks last), then newest (`PickOptions.created`), then by distance; `resolvePick` lists the bodies only where no object is (objects over a small far body are hidden and not pickable, `lib/disc-hide.ts`); `bodyHitRadiusPx` and `bodyPickExtra` make a drawn body pickable.
- `lib/entity-tree.ts`: body entities are the tree nodes (`TreeRow.entityId`); registry bodies without an entity keep a synthesised node. `lib/scope.ts`: star / planet / moon kinds are exempt from scope culling.

## Scene (bodies in the renderer)

- **Layer**: `scene/celestial.ts` `CelestialLayer` is fed by `setBodies(recs)` (SceneController.setEntities passes the records with `isBodyEntity`) and keeps a map keyed by registry id. Moon (IAU rotation, map or procedural) and Sun (emissive disc and glow) look and sit as before; Mars has its map; any other registry body is a sphere lit from the Sun as seen from that body, placed by `refDisplay` at its true position, in the colour of its entry in `lib/body-style.ts` (approximate display colours, not measurements; unknown bodies are neutral grey), oriented by its IAU rotation (pole on z). Gas and ice giants get a latitudinal band pattern in their own two colours (one procedural function in the body shader), solid bodies slight albedo noise. A body with a registry `flattening` (Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus, Neptune, Ceres; not the Moon or Mars, whose maps are sphere-based) is squashed along its pole by `1 - f` and carries that pole in `SceneBody.pole`, so the occluders, disc cones and picks use the same ellipsoid. A style with `rings` (Saturn: 74,500 to 136,800 km, NASA Saturn fact sheet; opacity profile C faint, B dense, Cassini division, A moderate) adds a double-sided, Sun-lit, semi-transparent ring mesh in the equatorial plane (log-depth chunks like every body material); the rings are not in `bodies()`, so they neither hide points nor take picks, and no ring or planet shadow is drawn. A body whose disc radius is under 2.5 px also gets a 9 px ring marker in its style colour (`CelestialLayer.updateMarkers`): one `THREE.Points` through the scene's point material, so the occluder slots hide it like any point, but with `aShow` = exempt, outside every point set (no scope thinning, no disc cone, not in RENDERED) and not pickable itself (the body's own pick extra covers it); it sits on the sight line 1.5 radii nearer than the centre so the body's own slot never hides it. The Earth is not drawn here (SceneController keeps its mesh group); the layer knows its centre, radius and flattening (`earth()`, `bodies()`).
- **Not points**: body entities are excluded from the SGP4 and dyn point sets (`satCount` unchanged), from `rebuildDyn` and from Fit all, but are in the graph. `worldPos(body entity)` is `refDisplay(registryId, t, scale).r`, so focus, follow, frame, the selection and orbit lines use the position of the mesh. `focus({id})` takes any entity id, a body entity (Mars and the Earth included) like any other; only a request without an id is the home view.
- **Occlusion**: one list, one test. `CelestialLayer.bodies()` is every drawn body (`SceneBody`, `lib/occlusion.ts`: centre, camera-relative centre `rel` in float64, radius, `flattening` for the Earth, the Earth's system reach); `prepareRender` hands it to an `OccluderSet`, which keeps at most `MAX_OCCLUDERS` (24) slots, the bodies with the largest angular radius first, skipping any whose apparent radius is under `MIN_OCCLUDER_PX` (0.25 px: it cannot hide a point visibly) and any the camera is inside; bodies beyond the 24th are counted in `overflow` / `maxOverflow`. The same slots are the point shader's uniform array (`uOccS`, `uOccE`, `uOccP` the pole axis, `uOccN`; `setOccluderUniforms`, mutated in place, no per-frame allocation) and the CPU test (`OccluderSet.hides`: picking, hover, labels, the RENDERED count, path picking). The test is one formula, `sphereOccludes` (CPU) and `bodyHides` (GLSL, line for line), a flattened body just a slot with `k = 1 / (1 - f)` stretching the body's pole axis (`v + (k - 1)(v . pole) pole`; +z for the Earth, so its path is unchanged; the test also compares a tilted oblate body); tests compare the float32 emulation of the shader with the CPU over a grid of positions for the Earth, Moon, Sun, Mars and a Jupiter. A marker behind Mars is therefore neither drawn nor pickable. Ties in angular radius keep registry order, so the choice is stable. The slot arrays are 3 x 24 = 72 uniform vectors; with the 8 cone slots (3 x 8 = 24), `uOccN`, `uPx` and three.js's 20 built-ins the point shader declares `POINT_UNIFORM_VECTORS` = 118 vertex uniform vectors, asserted against the 256 WebGL2 guarantees in `test/body-slots.test.ts` (the assumption is that minimum; real GPU limits were not queried).
- **Real positions for the bodies** (`apps/server/src/connectors/horizons-bodies.ts`, core `setBodyStream`): the registry holds an approximate Kepler orbit per body (planets: Standish mean elements, rotated from the ecliptic; dwarf planets: Horizons osculating elements at J2000; moons: orbits referred to the PARENT'S EQUATOR, `orbitPlane: 'equator'`, from the IAU pole via `equatorToInertialMatrix`, phase arbitrary). While the server is online the `horizons-bodies` connector fetches Horizons vector tables (centre = the parent's body centre) and puts them on the `body` provider as `ephemeris: {frame: '<PARENT>:inertial', samples}`; `BodyProvider` installs them with `setBodyStream`, which replaces the body's `Body.ephemeris` (Hermite inside the window, the Kepler orbit outside), so the planet, its moons and every frame hop through it agree. The Earth's own heliocentric position is `-sunPositionEci` (`EARTH.ephemeris`), so the planets are placed against the Sun the scene draws. Orbit lines keep their source: `bodyPeriodSec` from the registry elements and `stateIn` over that period; the streamed window is shorter than a revolution for the outer planets (Neptune: +-10 y of 165 y), so the rest of the loop is the Kepler orbit. Continuation rule (`setBodyStream`, `frames.ts`): outside the window the Kepler position is shifted by (streamed - Kepler) at the nearest window edge, in the stream's `<PARENT>:inertial` frame, weighted by `1 - smoothstep(dt / fade)` with `fade = STREAM_FADE_PERIOD_FRACTION` (0.25) of the body's period (one year for a body without a period), so position and velocity are continuous through the joint (the smoothstep has zero slope at both ends; measured jump across the edge for Mars, Ceres and Neptune with 3e5 to 6e6 km offsets is only the 2 ms of travel, under 0.05 km, velocity jump under 5e-9 km/s) and a quarter period on the error is the plain Kepler error again; a loop one period long closes within that error, not exactly.
- **Adding a body**: register it (`defaultBodies.register`, see the README), add a target in `horizons-bodies.ts` if Horizons has it, and give the `CelestialLayer` its entity; that is all. `bodies()` then carries it, so it occludes (shader and CPU), gets a disc cone when it is one of the 8 largest small discs (`discGroups`, `GROUP_SLOTS` = 8, ranked by apparent size), is pickable and labelled, with no other scene code; its look is one optional entry in `lib/body-style.ts` (colour, bands, noise, `rings: {innerKm, outerKm}`; a body without an entry is grey) (`test/occluders.test.ts` adds a Jupiter at 5e8 km and checks each). Past 24 bodies on screen at once, or 8 cones, raise `MAX_OCCLUDERS` / `GROUP_SLOTS` (`occluders.overflow` and the controller's `coneStats.overflow` count the losers; re-check the uniform budget test).
- **Picking** (`lib/body-scene.ts`): `bodyPickScan` (`bodyPickExtras` is its extras) builds one `PickExtra` per drawn body under the cursor by one precedence rule, `pickPrecedence` in `lib/pick.ts`: the cursor inside a body's disc (or its 5 px marker) belongs to that body unless a nearer, at-least-as-big disc also holds it; the wider hit area (14 px minimum, disc plus 3 px) applies only where no body's disc holds the cursor, so a moon's margin never takes a click from inside its planet's disc, while a moon in front of the planet is hit on its own marker and listed with it, planet first. The discs holding the cursor also go to `PickOptions.covers`: an object (a probe, a satellite) whose centre lies outside them is not a candidate there, so an object beside a planet cannot take a click aimed at its disc (hover uses the same rule). Earth included, with no ranks. A body behind another body (the Earth included, the same `sphereOccludes` test as the point shader) is dropped, and its own sphere is left out of its own test. `bodyPickExtra` also reports `discPx`, the projected disc radius without margin. `gatherWithBodies` lists objects (point, model, selection) by rank, then newest (`createdAt ?? updatedAt`), then distance, and `resolvePick` decides about the bodies: any object, the objects alone (a body never joins the objects of a click: the Earth's disc covers most of the screen when zoomed in, so every click on a satellite over it would open the popup); with no object, the bodies (one body is selected directly, several open the popup). Objects over a body whose disc radius is at most `GROUP_DISC_PX` (40 px) are not drawn and not candidates: `lib/disc-hide.ts` (`discGroups`, `hiddenByDisc`) builds the cone slots from the same `bodies()` list as the occluders: the candidates are the bodies with disc radius at most `GROUP_DISC_PX` and the camera outside, ranked by apparent disc radius, largest first, registry order on ties, and the first `GROUP_SLOTS` = 8 get a cone (near Jupiter the Galilean moons, from Earth the Moon, the Sun and the nearest planets; the rest are counted in `GroupStats.overflow` and keep their objects drawn), the point shader (`uGroup`/`uGroupD`/`uGroupE`) and `PickOptions.discHidden` apply the same test, and a point is hidden when it is inside the cone of the body's pick hit radius `max(14 px, disc + 3)` and in front of the body's centre plane (the Earth: or up to 42,164 km behind it, `EARTH_SYSTEM_DEPTH_KM`), or inside the shell about its centre (drawn radius + 3 px; the Earth: + 2000 km, `EARTH_SYSTEM_REACH_KM`, the LEO shell just past the limb). Points with `aShow = 2` (`SHOW_EXEMPT`: the selected, hovered and followed entity, launch vehicles, stages and capsules more than 10 km above the ground, the highlight set, the selected path's ticks) are exempt; related, filter-kept and labelled entities, ground stations and launch pads are hidden like the rest, and so are their labels (`SceneController.discHiddenPoint`). Hover (`nearestFirst`) names the nearest object. `pickMeta` builds the rank and creation maps (rebuilt after a catalog change). Labels follow the same idea (`LabelPlanner`, `lib/labels.ts`): among the body labels the bigger disc goes first, then the higher in the hierarchy (`add(..., size, rank, parent)`); `addDisc` registers every drawn body's disc, and a label (except the selected and the hovered one) whose anchor is inside a larger body's disc is dropped (objects over a backdrop disc beyond `BACKDROP_DISC_PX` = 120 keep theirs); a moon's label fades in between `MOON_SEP_MIN` and `MOON_SEP_FULL` (2 to 4 label heights) from its planet's limb.
- **Orbit lines** (`scene/overlays.ts`, `EntityOverlay.update`): body entities with `render.orbitLine` use the same orbit-line machinery (`orbitPeriodSec` gives their period, `frameForRec` the parent's frame), without footprint, trail or ground track. See "Orbit-line rule" below for which lines are drawn. The refresh runs when the selection, the catalog or the query changes. `PathOverlay` skips body entities. An orbit line stops at the first sample that is a jump (a step of a quarter of the radius or more), so the line of a launch vehicle on its modeled orbit ends where that orbit ends (12 h after SECO) instead of reaching the pad marker the vehicle returns to.

## Orbit-line rule

One universal rule decides which orbit lines are drawn, from one pure function (`orbitLines`, `lib/orbit-lines.ts`), and nothing else adds a line:

- **The selected entity's trajectory** is always drawn at full strength: its orbit line round its primary body (`bodyEntityId(rec.bodyId)`: an Earth satellite round the Earth, LRO round the Moon, the Moon round the Earth, the Earth and Mars round the Sun). A selected body without an orbit (the Sun, `render.orbitLine` unset) draws none.
- **The orbits of its children** are drawn DIMMED, like Highlight dims (`lib/dim.ts`: one `DIM` constant for the colour of a dimmed point and of a dimmed orbit line), with no trail, footprint or ground track. The children of a body are the body entities whose `parentId` is it (the Sun gives the planets, the Earth the Moon) and the objects whose primary `bodyId` is it (the Earth's satellites, the Moon's probes), those the data flags with `render.orbitLine` or that have an ephemeris or Kepler elements (probes; `sampled`, `keplerian`) and are on a closed orbit now. An object has no children. So the Sun dims the Earth's and Mars's orbits, the Earth draws its own orbit round the Sun and dims the Moon's and the satellites', the Moon draws its own and dims LRO's.
- **Nothing else.** No selection, no lines. The frame (locked or not), the camera, the distance and the clock do not change which lines exist: there is no frame-centre subject, no distance window, no list of "hinted" objects (the ISS is drawn when the Earth or the ISS is selected and not otherwise), no scope thinning. A hidden group (query) keeps no child line; the selected entity is always kept.
- **Cap and order.** A body can have thousands of children (the Earth's satellites), so at most `CHILD_ORBIT_LIMIT` = 12 child orbits are drawn: body entities first (`entityRank`: planets before moons), then objects flagged `render.orbitLine`, then the other candidates; inside each group `compareHierarchy` (newest `createdAt ?? updatedAt` first, then id). The order does not use the camera, so the set does not change while the user moves; the mass of unflagged SGP4 satellites is never a candidate.
- **The frame and the orbit.** An orbit line is never sampled in the frame, so a frame adds no second curve to it. The only thing the frame changes about the selected entity's lines is its **trail**: the tail of its motion (the last `trailSeconds`, at most a sixth of its revolution so it cannot become a second ellipse) seen from the frame of reference, `r(t_i) - c(t_i) + c(now)`. It is drawn only where it differs from the orbit: not when the frame centre is the entity's own primary body (the tail would lie on the orbit line: an Earth satellite in the Earth frame, LRO in the Moon frame) and not when the entity is itself the centre (`frameForRec` then falls back to the primary). A selected object without a closed orbit (a launch vehicle in ascent) has no orbit line and keeps its trajectory path in the frame (`PathOverlay`, `lib/paths.ts`). The footprint and the ground track of a selected Earth satellite are Earth surface features and ignore the frame.
- **Roles in the scene.** `SceneController.refreshOrbitLines` evaluates the function when the selection, the catalog or what the query shows changes (not per frame) and gives each `EntityOverlay` its role (`'selected'` or `'child'`, `EntityOverlay.update`). The earlier bugs this replaces: LRO showed its orbit round the Moon plus a trail relative to the locked object (the trail kept the frame while the line did not), and the ISS line was always drawn because the first six objects with `render.orbitLine` were hinted whatever was selected.

- **To scale**: every body line is the true orbit about its parent, drawn at the true distance like the bodies and probes: the Moon about the Earth, the Earth and Mars about the Sun (a plain circle through the displayed body, `EntityOverlay.buildOrbit`; it follows a moving centre by `c(now) - c(ref)`). There is no distance compression (the old compressed scale drew the Earth's line scaled about the Earth and gave Mars none, which also left Mars and its orbiters in different places). The Sun has no line.

## Entity classes and untracked selection

- **One rule** (`viewClass`, `lib/view-class.ts`) classifies by primary body and trajectory, never by name: `body`; `orbiter` (a spacecraft on a closed orbit round a body other than the Earth and the Sun: `bodyId` is that registry body and the track is not a surface point, e.g. MRO, LRO); `satellite` (Earth-bound satellite, station, capsule, or an Earth-bound probe under `NEAR_ORBIT_KM` = 100,000 km); `probe` (Sun-bound, an Earth-bound probe beyond that, rovers and landers: Voyager, JWST at L2, New Horizons); `other`.
- **Uses**: the fly-to distance of an orbiter is `settleDistance` of its radius from its primary (`settleView`, `lib/camera.ts`), as an Earth satellite uses its geocentric radius, so MRO is framed at 3,200 km round Mars, not at the 1e8 km of Mars from the Earth; `categoryOf` puts an orbiter in `satellites` (the kind stays `probe`, so `kind:probe` still lists it and `type:probes` does not); the surface view stays Earth-only.
- **Untracked selection** (`focusTarget`, `lib/focus-target.ts`): an entity with no state at the sim time keeps the selection and the detail panel, but the camera frames the nearest ancestor with a position: its `parentId` entity, else its primary body entity, upward, the Earth last. The telemetry tab says "Untracked: showing <name>". When the clock reaches the entity's data, `SceneController` re-focuses it and follows it.
