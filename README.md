# EyesInOrbit

Local space mission control. A Node aggregator polls public spaceflight feeds (CelesTrak, Launch Library 2, Spaceflight News, NASA DSN, JPL Horizons, wheretheiss.at, ISS Lightstreamer), caches them in SQLite, and streams one unified entity model to a three.js web client. Any tracked object can be watched live on the globe alongside its launch info, livestream, news and telemetry.

Monorepo (npm workspaces): `packages/core` (`@eio/core`, shared model), `apps/server` (`@eio/server`), `apps/web` (`@eio/web`).

## Features

- **Globe and tracking**: three.js scene with Earth, atmosphere, star field and terminator lighting. Thousands of satellites (SGP4 propagated in a Web Worker), stations, debris, launch pads, DSN ground stations and deep-space probes. Hover for a name tooltip, click to select, FOLLOW keeps the camera on the selection, EARTH VIEW / Esc resets. The Earth hides everything behind it (stars, satellites, paths, deep-space markers); picking ignores hidden points too.
- **Bodies are entities**: the Sun, Earth, Mars and the Moon are catalog entities (`body-sun`, `body-earth`, `body-mars`, `body-moon`; provider `body`, group `bodies`) next to satellites and probes, so they show up in search and filters, have a position and an orbit trajectory (the Moon around the Earth), and carry `createdAt`. The server's `bodies` connector registers them offline; the web app and the mock build the same records from `bodyEntityRecords()`. Convention: [`docs/CONTRACT.md`](docs/CONTRACT.md#entity-conventions-server-produces-web-consumes).
- **Camera** (see [Controls](#controls)): altitude-aware zoom, a surface-aligned view (centred on the selected object, level horizon, north-up; on by default near the Earth), and FIT / FIT VISIBLE that frame the selection with its related objects or everything the filters leave on screen.
- **Trajectory paths**: select an object that is not SGP4 and its path is drawn: landing boosters (ascent and landing arc), launch vehicles with trajectory files, relative objects (`relative` provider), Keplerian objects and Horizons-sampled probes. Past is dim, future is bright, the current position is the selection ring, and composite phase boundaries (pad, ascent, landing, landed) get a tick and a label. Windows: probes -1 d to +2 d, trajectories -5 min to +10 min, relative objects +/-3 min, Keplerian -1/4 to +1 period. A launch vehicle's or booster's planned flight is drawn whole from the pad whatever the clock says; for a launch that goes on in its modeled orbit (`on orbit (modeled)`, 12 h) the line carries on past SECO only for the first part of the orbit (the window is capped at 45 minutes from liftoff, about 26 minutes of orbit for Falcon 9), because the whole path is one polyline glued to the rotating Earth; the full orbit is the orbit line of the vehicle once it is in orbit (it stops where the modeled orbit ends) and of its payload entity. The HUD shows the window. SGP4 objects keep their orbit line, trail and ground track.
- <a id="frame-of-reference"></a>**Frame of reference**: the scene is Earth-inertial, but the *frame* is a centre, a body (Earth by default, Moon, Sun) or an entity. What the frame changes is everything that depends on time: an object's trail and path are sampled relative to the centre at each sample time (`r(t) - c(t)`) and drawn around the centre's current position, so a Moon orbiter closes around the Moon, an Earth satellite's trail seen from the Moon is its true relative curve, and with the Earth (default) nothing differs from before. **Orbit lines** do not depend on the frame or the lock: a selected object's orbit line is drawn round what it orbits (an Earth satellite round the Earth, LRO round the Moon), and selecting a body also draws the orbits of its child bodies (the Earth: its own orbit round the Sun and the Moon's round the Earth; the Sun: the Earth's and Mars's). In the compressed distance scale the Earth's orbit is drawn scaled to the displayed Sun distance (Mars's only in the true scale), see [`docs/frames.md`](docs/frames.md#orbit-line-rule). Readouts follow: the Telemetry tab shows range and speed relative to the centre (`Range from Moon`), and the orbit period uses the object's primary body (LRO: the Moon's gm, about 2 h). Selecting an object moves the camera as always and does not change the frame; the frame breadcrumb (top left of the scene, `SUN > EARTH > MOON > LRO`), the entity tree's body nodes and the body tabs set it (selecting a body entity such as `body-moon` does too, unless the frame is locked), EARTH VIEW / Esc reset it to the Earth and unlock it. The frame is remembered in this browser (`localStorage['eio.frame']`, the lock is not): after a reload it comes back once the catalog is loaded, falling back to the Earth when the stored centre no longer exists. A locked frame (the lock toggle in the breadcrumb, or LOCK FRAME TO THIS OBJECT in the detail panel; in code `setFrame(centre, {lock: true})`) is never changed by selecting objects, e.g. lock on the ISS, then pick other satellites to see their paths and range relative to the ISS (`CAM ... FROM ISS [LOCKED]`). The entity that is the centre is drawn relative to its own primary body. Footprints and ground tracks stay Earth features. Design and contract: [`docs/frames.md`](docs/frames.md); maths in `apps/web/src/lib/frame.ts`.
- **Search and filter**: debounced search by name, NORAD id or COSPAR id (server-ranked via `/api/search`, local ranking if the request fails), kind and group filter chips with counts, virtualised entity browser with a TREE / LIST toggle (see [Entity tree](#entity-tree)). Filters also hide non-matching objects in the scene.
- <a id="entity-tree"></a>**Entity tree**: the entity browser's default view (TREE; LIST is the flat list, the choice is remembered in this browser) groups objects under the body they orbit (`EntityRecord.bodyId`), nested like the bodies: `SUN > EARTH > (MOON > LRO, then the Earth's objects in kind groups: satellite, debris, ...)` and `SUN > MARS > rover`. Body nodes show how many objects they hold, below them and in their sub-bodies. Nodes start collapsed except the path to the frame of reference (the Earth: Sun and Earth open) and, while a search or filter is active, every node with a hit (the counts are those of the filtered set; bodies without a hit disappear). The arrow folds a node; the body nodes are the body entities (`body-sun`, `body-earth`, `body-mars`, `body-moon`): a click on a body name selects the body (the detail panel shows its radius, GM, orbital period and parent), makes it the frame of reference and flies there (Earth, Moon, Sun and Mars alike); a click on an object selects it exactly as in the list; the kind and group chips show the most common ones (10 kinds, 14 groups, the body kinds star, planet and moon always) and a `+n more` chip opens all of them in a scrollable strip; the object that is the frame is tagged `FRAME`. Built on the virtualised list: only open nodes become rows (one flattened array, one fixed-height row each, pure logic in `apps/web/src/lib/entity-tree.ts`), so the 12,000-object catalog costs one pass per change and a few dozen DOM rows.
- **Frame breadcrumb and lock**: the bar under the scene buttons shows the frame as a path from the Sun (`FRAME  SUN > EARTH > MOON > LRO`). Click a crumb to make it the frame and fly there (Mars, without a display, is greyed). The lock toggle (`LOCK` / `LOCKED`, amber when locked) keeps the frame while other objects are selected, e.g. lock the ISS and pick satellites to see their paths and range relative to it. In the detail panel, LOCK FRAME TO THIS OBJECT makes the selected object the frame and locks it (it reads UNLOCK FRAME while it is the locked frame). When the frame is an object with no state at the simulation time (outside its data range) the bar says so, because paths relative to it are then not drawn.
- **Launch filter**: a searchable list of launch groups (objects that share a launch, derived from the COSPAR designator `YYYY-NNN`, only groups with 2 or more members, largest first; type `2251`, `cosmos` or `1993-036`). Selecting one filters the entity list and the globe. SHOW ONLY hides everything else, HIGHLIGHT dims it; members are drawn larger. A chip shows the active group and clears it. Selecting a group also calls `runtime.onFitRequest?.(ids)` so the camera can frame the members once a fit handler is installed (none is wired yet). Example: pick COSMOS 2251 to see its debris cloud at once.
- **Detail panel** with tabs:
  - Telemetry: lat/lon/altitude, geocentric range, speed, period, phase, element age, confidence, attributes, identifiers; ISS live fix and telemetry; the current DSN link for DSN-tracked probes.
  - Launch (launch entities only): countdown, status, phase, window, provider, vehicle, mission, pad, and for Falcon 9 an [ascent telemetry](#ascent-telemetry) section (modeled or measured altitude, speed and downrange charts, event timeline).
  - Stream (when the entity or its launch has streams): YouTube links are embedded; NASA TV, X and other links open in a new tab. POP OUT floats a YouTube stream above the app (up to 3 windows; drag the title bar, resize from the corner at 16:9, DOCK returns it to the tab; positions are remembered). Hover a player for PLAY, PAUSE, MUTE and UNMUTE; the embeds hide what YouTube's parameters allow (see `apps/web/src/lib/youtube.ts`).
  - News: Spaceflight News articles for the object (`links.newsQuery` or name), or all spaceflight.
- **Launch board**: upcoming launches (plus the last 2 hours) with live countdowns, LL2 status and derived mission phase. Click a card to select the launch entity or fly to its pad. The small SKIP button on a card (and SKIP TO LAUNCH in the Launch tab) jumps the simulation clock to liftoff, see [Jumping to a launch](#jumping-to-a-launch).
- **DSN panel**: dishes with active links first, craft chips that select the spacecraft, range, round-trip light time, downlink band and rate. It is **hidden by default**: switch it on in the PANELS menu (top bar, see [Controls](#controls)). The DSN data keeps updating while it is hidden, and the entity detail's Telemetry tab still shows a probe's current DSN link.
- **PANELS menu**: the top bar button (icon below 1500 px wide, `PANELS` above) opens a popover with a checkbox for the DSN panel, the launch board and the news ticker. A hidden panel gives its space back (the globe grows; with the launch board and the DSN panel both hidden the whole bottom row goes, with only one of them shown it takes the full width), and showing it again restores its previous size. The choice is kept in `localStorage` (`eio.panels.v1`); defaults: DSN hidden, launch board and ticker shown.
- **News ticker**: scrolling global headlines, pauses on hover.
- **Time controls**: LIVE, PAUSE/PLAY, a REWIND toggle (negative rate at the selected speed), speeds x1/x10/x60/x600 and four numeric fields (`±dd:hh:mm:ss`) that show and set the offset of sim time from now (see [Controls](#controls)). All providers are evaluated at the simulation clock. Data windows limit what exists away from now: SGP4 objects extrapolate (accuracy falls off over days), Horizons probes cover -3 d to +7 d around the last fetch, and an entity without a state at the chosen time shows "No state available at this time" with its data range in the Telemetry tab.
- **Scope (what is drawn)**: the scene does not draw everything everywhere. With SCOPE AUTO (default) an object is drawn when it is within 80 camera distances of the camera focus (the camera target: the followed object, the surface point, or the Earth's centre), with a floor of 100 km: 4 km above a pad that keeps ~320 km around it (the pad complex, the booster; not the satellite catalogue or any probe), the default Earth view (24,000 km) keeps ~1.9 million km (JWST yes, the deep-space shell appears once the camera pulls out a little). Past the radius the satellite cloud is thinned over one more radius (a stable pseudo-random subset) and single entities are cut at 1.5 radii. Always kept: the selection, its related objects (the same lookup FIT uses), the hovered object and, while a kind/group/launch filter is on, everything that filter lets through. Objects behind the Earth are hidden exactly (see Draw order and depth), and out-of-scope objects can be neither hovered nor picked. The SCOPE button toggles AUTO / ALL (ALL draws everything the filters allow), and the HUD shows `RENDERED n / total` (in scope, not filtered out, inside the view and in front of the Earth, out of everything with a position). Policy maths: `lib/scope.ts`.
- **Labels**: names drawn over the scene (the LABELS button, on by default, remembered in `localStorage['eio.labels']`). Bodies (Sun, Earth, Moon, Mars: the body entities with `render.label: true`) are named when zoomed out: the label sits beside the disc, is full while the body's apparent radius is under 50 px, fades out linearly to nothing at 220 px (a body that fills the view needs no name, so the default 24,000 km Earth view has no Earth label) and is hidden when the body is behind the Earth, Moon, Sun or Mars. The selected object is always named; other entities are named only if they ask for it (`render.label: true`: the ISS, probes, launch vehicles, DSN stations), unless a kind / group / launch filter is on, in which case the objects it lets through are named too (satellite labels need a filter; search does not filter the scene). Priority: the selection, the hovered object, bodies, `render.label` entities, then filtered satellites, nearest the screen centre first (a label shown last frame keeps its place against a slightly nearer rival). At most 48 labels, a label overlapping a better one is dropped, names are cut at 28 characters. Candidates for filtered satellites are re-scanned about 7 times a second (the 96 nearest the screen centre per point set); every frame only those few are projected. The planner (`apps/web/src/lib/labels.ts`) is pure and tested; the DOM nodes are pooled (`apps/web/src/scene/LabelLayer.ts`), only changed properties are written.
- **Deep-space shell compression**: objects beyond 2,000,000 km from Earth (e.g. Voyager 1 at ~2.5e10 km) are drawn on a logarithmically compressed shell with direction preserved, and so are their paths. The HUD flags this ("distance not to scale"). Over a 3-day window a far probe's path is a few pixels at most (Voyager 1's is effectively a point); zoom in to see it.
- **True scale distance**: the HUD button next to that note switches DISTANCE from COMPRESSED (default) to TRUE SCALE. Probes are then drawn at their real geocentric positions (Voyager 1 at ~2.5e10 km, 167 AU), their paths at true scale (Voyager's 3 days are ~4.4 million km, visible once you zoom in), markers keep their screen size, and the camera may zoom out to 1e11 km (compressed: 3e7 km). Selecting a probe or FIT / FIT VISIBLE frame at those distances; the "distance not to scale" flag is shown only while something is on the compressed shell. The floating origin (positions are float64, camera-relative on the GPU) and the logarithmic depth buffer (far plane 1e13 km) carry the range; the near clamp (3 km above the ground, 0.2 km from a followed object) is unchanged. Switching re-frames a selected probe, and a free camera that was framing the shell returns to the Earth view.
- **ISS**: while the ISS is selected the client subscribes to the `iss` WebSocket topic and the server starts a 1 Hz wheretheiss.at fix (lat/lon/alt/velocity/visibility/footprint) and a Lightstreamer ISSLIVE telemetry feed (solar array joint angles, attitude quaternion, state vector). Both connectors are idle otherwise. See [ISS array telemetry](#iss-array-telemetry) for exactly what is verified.
- **3D models** (`apps/web/src/scene/models/`): entities whose `render.model` names a registered model are drawn as a true-size procedural three.js model instead of a point once the camera is near: `iss` (the integrated truss 108 m across with eight solar array wings on SARJ and BGA joints, radiators, the pressurized stack, Canadarm2; 73 m long), `satellite` (bus, two sun-tracking panels; one mock Starlink uses it) and `rocket-stage` (Falcon 9 first-stage proportions, legs, grid fins; launch boosters use it). There are no binary assets. Models are placed camera-relative in float64 like everything else (floating origin, log depth), so they stay steady at 25 m from the camera. They appear within 45 km (ISS), 12 km (stage) or 6 km (satellite); the point stays until the model is larger than it on screen (about 9 km for the ISS), then the point and, for a large model, the selection ring give way to the model; the model keeps the entity's picking, hover, scope and filter behaviour (a filter-dimmed entity gets a dimmed model). Orientation is computed from the state vector: the flight attitude LVLH (x along the velocity, z nadir, y completing) for stations and satellites, nose along the Earth-relative velocity for stages (upright on the pad and while landing); it rotates correctly in the inertial frame while you scrub. Lighting is the Sun direction plus a little ambient and earthshine, darkened by a conical Earth-shadow model. With no model in range the cost is one distance test per model entity. When the ISS arrays are live, the measured attitude quaternion replaces the plain LVLH attitude.
- **ISS solar arrays**: the model's two SARJ and eight BGA joints are driven by the live ISSLIVE angles when that is verified, fresh and the clock is live, else by a computed pose that points the arrays at the Sun (derived from the Sun vector in the ISS body frame). The Telemetry tab shows the source (`LIVE`, `COMPUTED` or `STATIC`) and an ARRAYS readout (SARJ port and starboard, the eight BGA angles); the HUD shows `MODEL ISS · ARRAYS ...`. Mock mode animates the arrays with the computed pose.
- **Health indicator**: top-right connection state (link up, reconnecting, offline, mock) and a per-connector popover with status, age, cache use and errors.
- **Offline behaviour**: `OFFLINE=1` serves only what the SQLite cache holds. `npm run assets` downloads the Earth map `earth-hires.jpg` (NASA Blue Marble resized from the 21600 x 10800 original to 8192 px, 4.9 km per texel, about 180 MB of video memory; a GPU with a smaller texture limit gets it scaled down to that limit) and the Moon map. It is the only Earth map: close views add the streamed DETAIL tiles, and there is no 2k fallback. The map is loaded once at page load, so reload the page after downloading it. The browser console says which map was used or why it was skipped. If you skip `npm run assets` (or the file is missing) the globe uses the procedural Earth. A stale `apps/web/public/textures/earth.jpg` from earlier versions is no longer read; it is git-ignored and can be deleted.
- **Mock mode**: add `?mock=1` to the web URL to run entirely on synthetic in-memory data (also used automatically when `/api/health` is unreachable). `?mock=1&n=12000` sets the number of mock satellites (default 2000) for stress tests.

## Controls

| Input | Action |
|---|---|
| Left drag | Orbit (surface view: look around the local horizon) |
| Right drag, Shift + drag | Pan. In the surface view it lets go of the followed object and slides the target east/north along the ground at the object's height |
| Wheel, pinch | Zoom. The step scales with the camera's height above the surface: fine near the ground, coarse in deep space. The camera stops 3 km above the ground (the Earth has no terrain), at most 30,000,000 km out (1e11 km in true-scale distance mode) |
| Click on a launch path | With a launch (or its booster) selected, hovering its drawn trajectory highlights it and shows `T+mm:ss` next to the cursor; a click jumps the simulation clock to that point of the flight at the current rate. Lowest priority: an object under the cursor and the choice list take the click first (only a body, such as the Earth's disc, gives way). Hit radius 8 px in screen space, so it works in any frame and view; orbit lines and other paths are not clickable. See [Jumping to a launch](#jumping-to-a-launch) |
| Click | Select an object (hover shows its name). If several objects lie within 10 px of the click (and are drawn: not filtered out, in scope, not behind the Earth, Moon or Sun), a list opens at the cursor instead, highest in the object hierarchy first (Sun, Earth, Mars, Moon, then objects by the body they orbit), then the most recently created, then the nearest to the cursor, with the kind's colour dot, name and kind (at most 12, then "+ n more"). Zoomed out (a body's disc under 40 px in radius, about 80 px across) the small objects over that body (inside its pick area, in front of it) are not drawn in 3D and not pickable, so a click there selects the body directly with no list; the selected, hovered, related and filter-kept objects, featured (labelled) entities, launch vehicles, pads and the selected path stay drawn and pickable, and two bodies under the cursor (an Earth disc with a Moon dot) still open the list. Zoomed in, a body is never listed beside the objects drawn over it. Hover names the nearest object, or the body when the crowd is hidden. While it is open the mouse wheel moves the highlight (the camera does not zoom), as do Up and Down; hovering a row highlights it; click a row or press Enter to select it; Esc, a click elsewhere or a camera drag closes it without selecting. The highlighted object gets the hover ring in the scene. A tap works the same (rows are tappable). Logic: `apps/web/src/lib/pick.ts`, popup: `ui/PickPopup.tsx` |
| FOLLOW | Keep the camera on the selection |
| `F` or FIT | Frame the selection and its related objects (bounding sphere, distance from the field of view, smooth fly-to): its `parentId` parent and children, relative-provider parents, a launch vehicle's pad, pads near a composite's launch point, the probes a DSN ground station is linked to; the selected object's drawn path is included |
| `Shift+F` or FIT VISIBLE | Frame everything the current filters leave visible |
| MODEL (shown when the selection has a 3D model) | Fly to the model and follow it (ISS 260 m, booster 120 m, satellite 40 m, from behind, beside and above on the lit side); `F` does the same when the selection has a model and nothing else to frame. While following, the zoom limit is 25 m for the ISS, 12 m for a stage or satellite |
| `S` or SURFACE | Toggle the surface-aligned view. The camera is centred on the selected object (or a focused launch pad, or the point under the view centre when nothing is followed) but oriented to the local surface: the local vertical is up, the view is north-up and the horizon stays level, and the Earth hides the stars below it. **On by default**: selecting or focusing anything near the Earth (an orbit, a launch ascent or booster, a pad, a landed booster, a ground station) switches the camera into it with the usual blend; deep-space objects, other bodies and the Moon and Sun views stay in the globe view. The camera backs off until the object and the ground below it fit (about 1,400 km for the ISS; at higher altitudes the ground leaves the frame), and the camera may go below a high object's height. `F` re-fits without leaving the view; deselecting the object returns to the globe view. Press `S` or SURFACE to switch to the globe view: the choice is remembered in this browser (`localStorage` key `eio.surface`) and nothing switches it on again by itself until you press `S` once more, which restores the default. Leaving flies back to the globe view of the same place |
| Time controls (top bar) | The chip shows `LIVE`, `PAUSED` or the direction and speed (`▶ ×60`, `◀ ×60`), next to the UTC sim time. **LIVE** returns to wall time at x1. **PAUSE / PLAY** stops time; PLAY resumes at the last chosen speed and direction. **◀◀ REW** is a toggle: time runs backwards at the selected speed (click again for forwards; while paused it only sets the direction PLAY will use). **×1 / ×10 / ×60 / ×600** choose the speed (sim seconds per second). The four fields `±dd:hh:mm:ss` show the sim time as an offset from the real time now and keep counting at any speed; click a field and type (digits only, Tab to the next one, a pasted `-1:02:03:04` fills all four), then Enter or leave the field to jump to now + that offset at the current speed and direction (paused stays paused). Empty fields count as 0 and fields may overflow (90 minutes). The `+`/`−` button flips the sign. Esc abandons an edit. While a field has focus the live value does not overwrite it. Reach is about +/- 100 years, but most data only covers a few days (see below) |
| SUN / EARTH / MARS / MOON tabs (top bar; generated from the body entities in hierarchy order, hidden below 1100 px) | Set the **frame of reference** to that body (a shortcut for `setFrame`, see [Frame of reference](#frame-of-reference)) and centre the view on it. The camera orbits the body, the HUD reads `CAM ... FROM MOON`, the detail panel adds the selection's range and speed relative to it, and trails and paths are drawn relative to it: Lunar Reconnaissance Orbiter is a closed ellipse around the Moon in the Moon frame (orbit lines stay round what the object orbits, in every frame). The Moon, the Sun and Mars are drawn by the body entities (`scene/celestial.ts`): the Moon and the Sun at true size and position (Moon map from `npm run assets`, procedural grey without it), Mars as a plain rust-tinted sphere lit from the Sun, on the deep-space shell and shrunk by the shell factor in the compressed scale (the camera view distance shrinks with it) and at true size and distance in the true scale. The bodies are clickable (see Click) and the selected body (with its child bodies), the one the camera is centred on and a non-Earth frame body get their orbit line: the Moon around the Earth in both scales, the Earth around the Sun in both (in the compressed DISTANCE scale scaled to the displayed Sun distance), Mars around the Sun only in the true scale (the shell flattens its heliocentric orbit). The Sun view zooms out to 600 million km. Surface view is Earth-only for now |
| Frame bar (scene, top left) | Breadcrumb `SUN > EARTH > MOON > LRO` of the frame of reference: click a crumb to set it and fly there. **LOCK** toggles the lock: a locked frame (amber) stays while you select other objects. The detail panel's LOCK FRAME TO THIS OBJECT locks the frame on the selection (UNLOCK FRAME releases the lock) |
| Entity browser, TREE / LIST | TREE (default) nests objects under their body: click the arrow to fold a node, a body name to make it the frame (flies there), an object to select it. LIST is the flat list. Search and the filter chips apply inside the tree and open the nodes with hits |
| `Esc` or EARTH VIEW | Deselect and return to the Earth view (also leaves the surface view and the Moon and Sun views, and sets the frame of reference back to the Earth, unlocked; this does not change the remembered `S` choice) |
| DISTANCE button (HUD, bottom) | Toggle COMPRESSED (default) / TRUE SCALE placement of objects beyond 2,000,000 km |
| DETAIL button | Close-up Earth imagery on or off (on by default, remembered in the browser). Below about 2,600 km the base map would be magnified, so a 4 x 4 window of finer tiles (NASA GIBS Blue Marble shaded relief and bathymetry, 2.4 km per pixel at level 4 down to 0.6 km at level 6) is streamed through the local server and blended over it. The HUD shows `DETAIL Z6 16/16` (level, tiles received); tiles that have not arrived show the base map. Tiles are cached in `data/imagery/` (400 MB cap, least recently used removed) and `OFFLINE=1` serves only what is cached |
| LABELS button | Show or hide the object labels (on by default, remembered in the browser): Sun, planets and moons named when zoomed out, the selection, and with a filter on the satellites it lets through (at most 48; see Labels above) |

| PANELS button (top bar) | Open the panels menu: tick or untick **DSN** (hidden by default), **LAUNCH BOARD** and **NEWS TICKER** to show or hide them. Hotkeys are ignored while typing in an input. Code can frame a set of entities with `runtime.onFitRequest?.(ids)` from `apps/web/src/state/runtime.ts` (set by `SceneView` while mounted).

## Quick start

Requires **Node 24** (the SQLite cache uses the built-in `node:sqlite`).

```
npm install
npm run assets     # optional: downloads the Earth (8192 px) and Moon maps (procedural Earth is used without them)
npm run dev        # server on :8787, web on :5173
npm test           # vitest, all workspaces
```

Open http://localhost:5173. The Vite dev server proxies `/api` and `/stream` to `localhost:8787` (set by `EIO_SERVER`, default `localhost:8787`, in `apps/web/vite.config.ts`; start Vite with `EIO_SERVER=localhost:8799` for a server on another `PORT`).

Other scripts: `npm run build` (production build of the web app into `apps/web/dist`; the server does not serve it), `npm run typecheck`, `npm run start -w @eio/server` (server without file watching).

## Configuration

All variables are optional. Copy `.env.example` to `.env` (git-ignored, read from the repo root or cwd; already-set environment variables win).

| Variable | Default | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| `PORT` | `8787` | HTTP and WebSocket port |
| `HOST` | `localhost` | Bind address (loopback only by default) |
| `LL2_TOKEN` | unset | Launch Library 2 token; raises the ~15 requests/hour anonymous limit |
| `LL2_BASE` | `https://ll.thespacedevs.com/2.2.0` | LL2 base URL |
| `DATA_DIR` | `<repo>/data` | Location of `cache.sqlite` and `trajectories/` |
| `OFFLINE` | `0` | `1` (or `true`) disables all network access; connectors serve the cache |
| `DISABLED_CONNECTORS` | unset | Comma-separated connector ids that are not started at all (`celestrak,launchlibrary,snapi,dsn,horizons,iss-rest,iss-live,flightclub,bodies`); they show as `disabled` in `/api/health`. Unlike `OFFLINE` the other connectors keep their network access, e.g. `DISABLED_CONNECTORS=celestrak,launchlibrary,snapi,dsn,horizons` while CelesTrak rate-limits you but the ISS feeds should still run |
| `DSN_URL` | NASA Eyes `dsn.xml` URLs | Comma-separated DSN Now XML candidates, tried in order |

### Amateur and navigation satellites

- **Navigation (MEO)**: CelesTrak groups `gps-ops` (GPS operational; there is no group named `gps`), `glo-ops` (GLONASS), `galileo`, `beidou` are fetched like the other groups and shown in the navigation colour (legend "Navigation"). They are medium-Earth-orbit sets (about 12 to 14 h periods), which satellite.js propagates with SDP4; core tests cover a GPS-like and a GEO set. CelesTrak also offers one combined `gnss` group (one download instead of four) that is not used because the constellations are separate chips.
- **Adding groups raises 403 exposure**: each group is one more download from CelesTrak, which asks clients not to fetch every group and blocks repeated downloads. Each group keeps its own cache (TTL 3 h) and its own backoff gate, so a blocked group never delays the others, and the five new groups are fetched last. For every new group a hand-saved `data/celestrak/<group>.json` (from `https://celestrak.org/NORAD/elements/gp.php?GROUP=<group>&FORMAT=json`) works as the fallback. If the extra downloads are a problem, remove groups from `FETCH_ORDER` in `connectors/celestrak.ts`.
- **Amateur radio**: CelesTrak group `amateur` (about 100 objects) gives the element sets; they stay in the plain satellite colour and are selected through their group. The group names were checked against third-party listings of CelesTrak's current-data page (`galileo`, `gps-ops` also on CelesTrak's own pages); CelesTrak itself could not be reached from the research environment.
- **SatNOGS DB** (<https://db.satnogs.org/api/>, CC BY-SA 4.0): read access needs no token. `GET /api/satellites/?format=json` lists satellites (`sat_id`, `norad_cat_id`, `name`, `names`, `status` such as `in orbit` or `re-entered`, `launched`, `operator`, `countries`, `reception_status`); `GET /api/transmitters/?format=json&satellite__norad_cat_id=<id>` lists frequencies and modes (`description`, `alive`, `uplink_low`, `downlink_low`, `mode`, `status`, `service`); `/api/tle/` serves element sets but CelesTrak already provides these and also has a `satnogs` group of the same objects. Anonymous throttling is not documented for DB (the SatNOGS Network observations API allows 60 requests per hour anonymous, 240 with a token). A connector that adds transmitter/status `attrs` to the `amateur` entities is not built: the hub replaces whole records on upsert, so the celestrak connector would have to merge the SatNOGS data itself. Other feeds: N2YO needs an API key and has its own request limits; AMSAT's status pages are HTML only.

## API

REST under `/api` (health, entities, entity by id, search, launches, news, dsn, iss, bodies, telemetry, credentials, imagery tiles) and a JSON WebSocket at `/stream` that pushes `entities.changed`, `launches`, `news`, `dsn`, `health` and, for subscribers of the `iss` topic, `iss.fix`, `iss.telemetry` and `iss.arrays` (`GET /api/iss` returns `{fix, telemetry, arrays}`). Routes, query parameters, message types and entity conventions are specified in [`docs/CONTRACT.md`](docs/CONTRACT.md); shared types are in `packages/core/src/{types,feeds}.ts`.

### API keys and logins in the UI

The **KEYS** button in the top bar opens a panel for the optional credentials: the Space-Track username and password and the Launch Library 2 token. Saving sends them to the local server (`PUT /api/credentials`, accepted from localhost only), which keeps them in `data/cache.sqlite` (plain text, like `.env`; the file is git-ignored) and uses them for its own requests. They take effect immediately, without a restart: a connector that was waiting for credentials starts and runs once, and changing the Space-Track login drops its cached elements and retry gate so the new login is tried at once. Values typed here override `.env`; clearing one falls back to `.env`. Secrets are write-only: `GET /api/credentials` reports only whether one is set and where it came from (`ui` or `env`), and the username is the one value shown back. The browser never calls Space-Track or Launch Library itself, because those services do not allow browser-origin requests with a login.

## Trajectory files

Drop ascent profiles into `data/trajectories/` as `*.json` or `*.csv`. The server rescans every 30 s; a matching launch becomes a `composite` entity (geodetic at the pad until NET, then a `sampled` ascent in `EARTH:fixed`). Files with errors are skipped and reported in the `flightclub` connector note. `*.local.*` files are git-ignored. A working example is `data/trajectories/example.json` (illustrative, matches no real launch).

JSON:

```json
{
  "id": "my-ascent",
  "source": "free text",
  "azimuthDeg": 90,
  "match": { "launchId": "<LL2 launch uuid>", "nameContains": "Starlink" },
  "rows": [
    { "tSec": 0,  "altKm": 0,  "speedKmS": 0.0 },
    { "tSec": 60, "altKm": 9,  "speedKmS": 0.45 }
  ]
}
```

CSV: `# key: value` comment lines (`id`, `source`, `azimuthDeg`, `launchId`, `nameContains`), then a header `tSec,altKm,speedKmS[,downrangeKm]` and one row per sample.

Rules (see the format comment at the top of `apps/server/src/trajectory.ts`):
- `match` needs `launchId` (exact LL2 id) or `nameContains` (case-insensitive substring of the launch name); `launchId` is tried first.
- At least 2 rows; `tSec` is seconds since liftoff, anchored at the launch NET.
- Every row needs either `downrangeKm`, or `speedKmS` (downrange is then integrated from speed and altitude rate).
- `azimuthDeg` defaults to 90 (due east).

## Jumping to a launch

All three only move the shared simulation clock (`clock.seek`); nothing is loaded, so they work for past and future launches alike, and a launch whose entity has no data at that time simply shows nothing in the scene.

- **SKIP TO LAUNCH** (launch board card: `SKIP`; Launch tab): seek to T-0 of the launch, its NET as the server last published it (read at the click, so a hold or a new NET is followed; a TBD / TBC launch is jumped to its placeholder NET, which the tooltip says). It plays from there: live and paused become x1, a forward scrub keeps its speed, a rewind turns forward.
- **Event rows and charts** (Launch tab, ascent telemetry): clicking a row of the events table, an event label on the timeline, or anywhere in a chart or the timeline jumps to `liftoff + T+`. The chart click is clamped to the charted range. These keep the rate: paused stays paused, a rewind keeps rewinding, live becomes x1.
- **The trajectory in the scene** (see the Click row of the controls table): the time of the nearest point of the drawn ascent or booster-return path, interpolated between samples.

Pure logic: `apps/web/src/lib/launch-seek.ts` (T-0, event time, rates, tooltips), `lib/path-pick.ts` (nearest point of a polyline in screen space, perspective-correct, clipped at the camera plane); the clock access is `state/seek.ts`.

## Ascent telemetry

Launch entities of **Falcon 9** launches (not Falcon Heavy) and **Electron** launches get an ascent path and an event timeline even though no open ascent telemetry exists (reference doc, section 2). Three sources, in order of precedence, reported as `attrs.trajectorySource`:

| `trajectorySource` | What it is | Measured? |
|---|---|---|
| `live` | rows posted to `POST /api/telemetry/:launchId` | yes, as accurate as whoever posts them |
| `file` | a file in `data/trajectories/` (Flight Club export, hand-made) | no |
| `modeled` | the parametric nominal ascent in `packages/core/src/ascent-model.ts` | **no, a model** |

**Modeled ascent.** Built from typical Falcon 9 / Starlink webcast figures: max-Q at about T+1:10 (12 km, 0.42 km/s), MECO at about T+2:30 (68 km, 2.3 km/s Earth-relative), stage separation 3 s and second-stage ignition 8 s after MECO, fairing at T+3:30, SECO-1 at about T+8:30 (165 km, about 7.6 km/s), booster entry burn, landing burn and touchdown (droneship about T+8:20, RTLS about T+7:45; the recovery plan comes from LL2 `launcher_stage[0].landing`). The launch azimuth follows from the target inclination and pad latitude (`sin Az = cos i / cos lat`, southward branch for Vandenberg, corrected for the Earth's rotation); the inclination comes from the launch description, a per-Starlink-group table (group 15 is the 70 degree shell) or a per-orbit and per-site default, and the used value is shown in the Launch tab. Downrange is integrated from speed and altitude rate with `reconstructDownrange`. After SECO the state is held for 10 minutes (a placeholder; the real orbit rises towards apogee, phase `orbit (held)`, until T+1110 s for Falcon 9, where the mission phase also turns ON_ORBIT). From there the vehicle follows a two-body orbit through that state for about 12 hours (phase `on orbit (modeled)`; no J2, no drag, no maneuvers; a placeholder until the payload is in a TLE feed) and then returns to the pad marker, and a `launch-<id>-payload` satellite entity follows the same orbit. Launches reported as failed skip the orbit and return to the pad marker after the ascent. Real flights differ by tens of seconds and a few percent. Everything in the UI is labelled MODELED until real rows arrive.

**Vehicles.** `VEHICLES` in `packages/core/src/ascent-model.ts` lists the vehicle profiles; `vehicleProfileFor` picks one from the LL2 vehicle and launch name. Falcon 9 is the reference (and the only one with a booster return); Electron uses the same ascent shape with its own rough timeline (MECO about T+2:30 at 78 km, SECO about T+8:55 into 190 x 520 km, figures from public webcast timelines and unverified, flagged as such in the Launch tab's assumptions). Vehicles that are not listed (Falcon Heavy, Ariane 6, Atlas V, Vulcan, Starship, Long March, ...) stay at the pad: their ascents (side boosters, long core burns, Starship's suborbital arc) look nothing like this shape and a profile would be misleading. To add a vehicle with a Falcon-9-like two-stage ascent, add a `VehicleProfile` (name regex, `recoverable`, `timeline`, assumption text) to `VEHICLES` and `VEHICLE_LIST`; the model, the chart, the events and the entity pick it up.

**Where launch trajectories can come from** (what each source gives and what the app does with it):

| Source | What it provides | Status here |
|---|---|---|
| Measured webcast overlays (SpaceX, Rocket Lab and others show T+, altitude, speed) | the only real ascent telemetry | typed or scripted into `POST /api/telemetry/:launchId`; reading the webcast automatically (OCR) is a stub |
| Flight Club (flightclub.io) simulations | community-simulated profiles for many vehicles, no stable public API | export by hand into `data/trajectories/` (see Trajectory files) |
| Launch Library 2 | pad, window, target orbit, recovery plan; no trajectories | used for the pad, azimuth, inclination and booster plan |
| Operator press kits and mission pages | typical event times (MECO, fairing, SECO) | the numbers behind the vehicle profiles; entered by hand |
| Space-Track / CelesTrak | the payload's real orbit once it is catalogued, hours after launch | used for the satellites, not for the ascent |
| FAA and NGA hazard areas (NOTAMs) | keep-out areas along the ground track | not implemented (the polygon parser is on hold) |

**Modeled booster return.** A recovered first stage also gets its own entity, `launch-<id>-booster` (kind `stage`, `parentId` the launch, the rocket-stage model), and a `booster` track in `GET /api/telemetry/:launchId` (`modeled.booster`). It shares the vehicle's ascent up to stage separation and then follows a kinematic reconstruction in the launch plane (`packages/core/src/booster.ts`): a ballistic coast (apogee about 125 km), a boostback burn (RTLS) or a short trim (droneship) that sets the horizontal speed, a coast to the entry burn, a cubic descent to the landing-burn start (0.3 km/s, 3.75 km) and a constant-deceleration landing burn. The horizontal speed between the entry burn and the landing burn is not a chosen curve: drag and thrust act along the velocity, so it falls as exp(-integral of k) with k = rho(z)·|v|/(2·beta) plus T/(m·|v|) during the entry burn (beta = m/(Cd·A), the standard-atmosphere density `airDensity`, and the vehicle's `booster` physics: mass, drag area, Cd, entry-burn thrust and length, `FALCON9_BOOSTER` for Falcon 9). With the rough Falcon 9 figures about 5 to 8 percent of the held horizontal speed is left at the landing burn, and the terminal velocity at its altitude (0.21 km/s) is close to the assumed 0.3 km/s. Mass, area and Cd are rough, so treat the shape as plausible rather than accurate. The held horizontal speed is solved so the touchdown lands on the target: 8 km from the pad for RTLS and 600 km for a droneship, both along the launch azimuth (LL2 gives only the landing location's abbreviation, not coordinates, so the droneship's real position is not used). The booster events (`BOOSTBACK`, `ENTRY_BURN`, `LANDING_BURN`, `LANDING`) now carry an altitude and speed from this track. Timing constants live in `BOOSTER_SHAPE`; nothing about the path is measured. There is no booster entity when a trajectory file supplies the vehicle path.

**Ingest.** Send measured rows (for example from a webcast OCR script or typed by hand):

```
curl -X POST http://localhost:8787/api/telemetry/<LL2 launch id> -H "content-type: application/json" -d '[
  {"tSec": 0,  "altKm": 0,    "speedKmS": 0},
  {"tSec": 60, "altKm": 8.4,  "speedKmS": 0.38},
  {"tSec": 120,"altKm": 38.0, "speedKmS": 1.25}
]'
```

`tSec` is seconds since liftoff (T+0 is the launch NET), `altKm` kilometres, `speedKmS` the Earth-relative speed in km/s (convert km/h by dividing by 3600). Rows are validated (finite numbers, `tSec` 0..7200, `altKm` -0.5..2000, `speedKmS` 0..15, at most 2000 rows per request, 7200 stored) and merged by `tSec`, so a script can post a row or a batch every second; add `"replace": true` to start over. The object form `{"rows": [...], "events": [{"name": "MECO", "tSec": 151}], "azimuthDeg": 158, "source": "webcast OCR", "replace": false}` also reports events (names `A-Z0-9_`, e.g. `MECO`, `SECO`, `STAGE_SEP`, `LANDING`; they replace the modeled time of the same name) and an azimuth (default: the modeled one). Downrange is integrated from the rows; the path is measured up to the last row and then follows the modeled shape re-anchored at the last measured point (reported as `liveExtrapolatedFromSec` and a lower sample `confidence`), or holds the last fix when the launch has no model. The call answers with `{ok, source, rows, lastTSec, extrapolatedFromSec}` and the server pushes `entities.changed`. `DELETE /api/telemetry/:launchId` clears the series. Posting and deleting are accepted from localhost sockets only (and only without a foreign `Origin`); rows are kept in memory and in the SQLite cache (7 days) so they survive a restart. `GET /api/telemetry/:launchId` returns `{launchId, entityId, liftoffMs, source, modeled, file, live}` (the modeled series with events and phases next to the posted rows).

**UI.** The Launch tab ends with an ASCENT TELEMETRY section: altitude, speed and downrange charts against T+ (modeled dashed once measured rows exist, measured solid), a cursor that follows the simulation clock (scrub, pause, rate), event markers (MAX-Q, MECO, SEP, SES, FAIRING, SECO and the booster ENTRY, LAND BURN, LANDING) with the current phase highlighted, readouts at the cursor, an events table and a data table. Before liftoff it shows the countdown and the nominal MAX-Q, MECO and SECO values. On the globe the same path is drawn by the usual path overlay with phase labels (stage 1 ascent, staging, stage 2 burn, orbit (held), on orbit (modeled)). Try it offline with `?mock=1` (a Starlink Group 15-15 launch from Vandenberg about 40 minutes ahead): scrub to its NET plus 4 minutes. The mock's Starlink Group 10-31 launch flew 3 hours ago: select it (or its `(payload)` satellite, listed under Earth) to see the modeled orbit; scrub a launch past T+1110 s and the board flips to ON ORBIT while the vehicle carries on along the orbit instead of returning to the pad, which it does only when the 12 hours of the modeled orbit are over (a late jump to the pad marker; the payload then has no position).

**Limits.** There is no OCR here (the `webcast-ocr` connector stays a stub). The model knows nothing about the actual payload mass, trajectory shaping or weather, its inclination for a given mission can be wrong when the provider data does not state it, and non-Falcon vehicles have no modeled ascent (they stay at the pad unless a trajectory file or posted rows exist). A launch id that LL2 no longer lists (older than the previous-10 window) cannot receive rows.

## Architecture

```
connectors -> Hub (EntityGraph + launches/news/dsn/iss state) -> REST + WebSocket -> web store -> scene / panels
     |
  SQLite kv cache (TTL, stale fallback)
```

- **Server** (`apps/server/src`): Fastify with `@fastify/compress`, `@fastify/cors` and `@fastify/websocket`. `main.ts` builds the connector list and the `Scheduler` (`connector.ts`): jittered starts, per-connector timers, exponential backoff, topic gating, health records. `http.ts` is the fetch layer (`cachedFetch`: fresh cache hit, else network, else stale cache). `store.ts` is the SQLite key/value cache (entries expired for more than 30 days are pruned at startup). `hub.ts` holds shared state and debounces `entities.changed` (250 ms).
- **Core** (`packages/core/src`): body-agnostic. Bodies (`SUN`, `EARTH`, `MOON`, `MARS`) and frames `<BODY>:inertial|fixed`, state providers (`sgp4`, `sampled`, `keplerian`, `geodetic`, `relative`, `composite`), the `EntityGraph` that resolves parents and converts frames, the mission state machine (`deriveMissionState`), the simulation `Clock`, and ascent helpers. Units are km, km/s and ms since epoch.
- **Web** (`apps/web/src`): React 18 + zustand store (`state/store.ts`). The raw three.js `SceneController` (`scene/`) is not wrapped in r3f. SGP4 entities propagate in `workers/propagator.worker.ts` into a transferable `Float32Array`; other providers are evaluated on the main thread through an `EntityGraph` (`state/runtime.ts`). `api/client.ts` chooses the REST client or the mock API (`mock/`).
- **Models**: `scene/models/registry.ts` maps `render.model` keys to `ModelDef`s (true-size bounds, LOD distance, minimum follow distance, FIT distance, attitude mode, builder). Builders (`iss.ts`, `satellite.ts`, `rocket-stage.ts`) use the primitive kit in `kit.ts` (shared dimmable materials, named joints, a canvas-drawn solar cell texture) in metres in a body frame (+X forward, +Y starboard, +Z nadir). `ModelLayer` owns the instances: each frame, for the few model entities, it takes the cheap point-buffer position, rejects anything beyond the LOD distance, else resolves the exact state, subtracts the camera in float64, applies the LOD rules (`lib/model-lod.ts`), orients the model (`lib/attitude.ts`) and poses its joints (`lib/arrays.ts` for the ISS). `SceneController` only feeds it positions, visibility, scope and joint callbacks and hides the replaced sprites.
- **Frame of reference**: store `frame` (`Centre`: body or entity), `frameLocked`, `setFrame(centre, {lock?, fly?})`, `setFrameLocked`, `useFrameInfo()` (label, breadcrumb crumbs). `lib/frame.ts` holds the pure maths (`centrePositionEci`, `relativeState`, `framePathPoint` = r(t_i) - c(t_i) + c(ref), labels). `samplePath` and `EntityOverlay` take a `FrameSampler`; the buffers are built around the centre at build time and `RelPositions.shift` translates them by c(now) - ref each frame, so they follow a moving centre without rebuilds (rebuilt when the centre, the distance scale or the window changes). `SceneController.setFrame` mirrors the store; `focus({centre})` pivots the camera (a body through the body view, an entity through the follow machinery).
- **Paths and frames**: `lib/paths.ts` samples `provider.stateAt` over a window (<= ~480 points plus exact composite phase boundaries, buffers reused, rebuilt only when the window has slid a tenth of its length). Everything is drawn in the Earth-inertial scene, with one exception: when every sample is natively `EARTH:fixed` and the window is <= 45 min (the booster, launch ascents), or the plan is a whole planned launch flight (anchored) whose keplerian orbit tail is converted to `EARTH:fixed` sample by sample, the path stays in Earth-fixed coordinates and is rotated by the Earth's spin each frame, so a booster standing on its landing pad does not draw a 460 m/s eastward streak; the marker, evaluated at the current time, still sits on it. Inertial points get the deep-space shell compression. Path buffers are float64.
- **Floating origin (precision)**: all positions are kept in float64 on the CPU (the SGP4 worker also returns float64) and the camera position is subtracted there; the GPU only ever sees camera-relative coordinates (`scene/relative.ts`, `lib/floating-origin.ts`). The render camera sits at the origin, the Earth and atmosphere objects are moved to minus the camera position. This is what keeps objects and trajectories steady when the camera is hundreds of metres from them (a float32 holds 0.5 m at Earth radius). Everything in one frame, including the followed anchor, is evaluated at one simulation time.
- **Draw order and depth**: the renderer uses a logarithmic depth buffer (depth precision is uniform in log distance, near 5 m, far 1e13 km). Earth, atmosphere, paths and orbit lines are depth tested. Marker sprites (satellites, probes, pads, selection ring) are not: a sprite has a single depth for all its pixels and was half cut by the Earth at the limb. Their vertex shader instead hides a marker whose line of sight crosses the Earth ellipsoid or the displayed Moon or Sun sphere (`lib/occlusion.ts`, same maths on the CPU for picking, the RENDERED count and path labels; the spheres use the displayed centre and radius, so they follow the DISTANCE scale), so markers never clip into the ground at any rotation or zoom and ground markers need no lift (paths and markers are nudged 5 m up). Stars write depth 1.0, so the opaque Earth and everything in front of the far plane occludes them.

## Extending

- **New data feed**: add a file in `apps/server/src/connectors/` exporting a `Connector` (`id`, optional `intervalMs`, `startDelayMs`, `topic`, `network: false`, `start`, `run`, `stop`, or `stub`). Use `ctx.cachedFetch(key, url, ttlMs, parse)` for network access and `ctx.hub.upsert(...)` / `hub.setLaunches` / `hub.setDsn` etc. to publish. Add it to the `CONNECTORS` array in `apps/server/src/main.ts`. Throw from `run` to report an error and back off; call `ctx.softFail(msg, retryMs)` for a partial failure.
- **New entity kind** (rover, drone, lander): emit an `EntityRecord` with any `kind` string and a provider spec; no core change is needed. Optional `render` hints set `glyph` (`dot|diamond|ring|triangle|square`), `colorKey`, `trailSeconds`, `footprint`, `orbitLine` and `label`. For a colour that does not exist yet, extend `colorKey` in `packages/core/src/types.ts`, then `KIND_COLOR` in `apps/web/src/lib/entity.ts`, `POINT_SIZE`/`FALLBACK` in `apps/web/src/scene/palette.ts` and a `--c-<key>` variable in `apps/web/src/styles.css`.
- **Entity that changes phase** (a landing booster): use a `composite` provider chaining `sampled` ascent, `relative` landing arc and `geodetic` landed segments. The launch ascent composites (`packages/core/src/ascent-model.ts`) and the booster composites in `apps/server/src/connectors/launchlibrary.ts` are complete examples.
- **New 3D model**: write a builder returning a `ModelInstance` (use `ModelKit` in `apps/web/src/scene/models/kit.ts`: metres, +X forward, +Y starboard, +Z nadir, optional named joints) and register its `ModelDef` in `scene/models/registry.ts`; then set `render.model` to its key on any entity. Entities need a provider with a state (a velocity gives the orientation); unknown keys are ignored.
- **New body**: define a `Body` (radius, flattening, GM, rotation, approximate orbit around its parent) in `packages/core/src/bodies.ts` and add it to the default list in `BodyRegistry`'s constructor (or call `defaultBodies.register`). Frames `<BODY>:inertial|fixed` and conversions then work for it, and `/api/bodies` lists it. The top bar's body tabs, the entity tree's body nodes and the breadcrumb come from the body entities, so the new body shows up there without UI work (the scene still needs to draw it).
- **New state provider**: add its spec to the `ProviderSpec` union in `packages/core/src/types.ts`, implement `StateProvider` (`stateAt`, `toSpec`) in `packages/core/src/providers/`, then add a `case` in `providerFromSpec` and an export in `providers/index.ts`. Server and web both rebuild providers through that function.

## Layout

- **Resizing**: drag the borders between the entity list and the globe, the globe and the detail panel, the globe and the bottom row, and the launch board and the DSN panel (while both are shown). Double-click a border (or press Enter on it) to reset it to its default. Borders are keyboard focusable: arrow keys move by 16 px (Shift: 64 px), Home/End jump to the minimum/maximum. Sizes are clamped (the globe always keeps at least 360 x 200 px) and re-clamped when the window shrinks.
- **Optional panels**: the PANELS menu in the top bar shows and hides the DSN panel (hidden by default), the launch board and the news ticker, see [Controls](#controls). The grid reclaims the space (`--ticker-h` and `--bottom-h` become 0, a `bottom solo` row when only one of the two bottom panels is shown), the splitters that no longer have two neighbours are not rendered, and the sizes you set stay stored, so a panel comes back at its previous size. State and pure parse/serialise logic: `state/panels.ts` (`usePanels`, `setPanel('dsn', true)` opens a panel from code); menu: `ui/PanelsMenu.tsx`.
- **Persistence**: sizes are stored in `localStorage` under `eio.layout.v1` (per browser; a default size is simply not stored). If storage is unavailable the layout works but is not remembered. At 820 px and narrower the page stacks vertically and the borders are hidden.
- **Implementation**: sizes are CSS variables (`--left-w`, `--right-w`, `--bottom-h`, `--dsn-w`) on `.app` set by `ui/ResizableLayout.tsx`; `ui/Splitter.tsx` is the reusable pointer-capture splitter; pure clamp and persistence logic is in `lib/layout.ts`. The three.js canvas follows through the `ResizeObserver` in `SceneController`, which resizes the drawing buffer and redraws synchronously (before the browser paints), so dragging a border does not flash a blank globe; the canvas CSS size is 100% of its container.
- **Displayed values**: all number formatting goes through `lib/format.ts` with fixed decimals per unit (lat/lon 3, altitude 1, distances by unit band (km, Mkm, AU), speed 3 km/s or 0 km/h, period in minutes 1, light time 1 s, data rates scaled to b/s, kb/s, Mb/s, Gb/s). Server `attrs` are formatted by key (`rangeKm`, `rtltSec`, `dataRateBps`, ...) and any other long decimal inside attribute or telemetry text is rounded to 3 decimals. Numeric cells use tabular digits and stay on one line (ellipsis when too narrow); only ids and URLs may break anywhere.

## CelesTrak blocked or unreachable

- **Symptom**: the `celestrak` connector reports `fetch failed (UND_ERR_CONNECT_TIMEOUT)` (also `ECONNRESET`, `ETIMEDOUT`), only `stations` or nothing loads, and the health note says `skipped N groups (...)`. That is a TCP connect timeout to `celestrak.org:443`, not an HTTP 403: other hosts work, and the app cannot see a rate limit at that level. It was seen while CelesTrak itself was down (confirmed server-side on 2026-10-24); an IP-level block or an egress problem looks identical from here, so the probe below is the first thing to run. A connect error stops the run at the first failure instead of trying all 11 groups and keeps the short retry schedule (30 min, doubling up to 12 h), because an outage usually ends on its own; HTTP 403/429 wait 3 h first and `Retry-After` is honoured. Downloads are spaced 1 to 2 s apart.
- **Probe**: `npm run celestrak:probe` (or `npm run celestrak:probe -- gps-ops` for another group) makes exactly one request with the server's headers and a 20 s timeout and prints the status, the response headers (`server`, `cf-ray`, `retry-after`), the first 300 body characters, or the error with its cause code. A connect timeout there confirms the network problem; a 403 with a body is a real rate limit.
- **What to try**: another network or a phone hotspot; save the group JSON from a browser on a network that works (`https://celestrak.org/NORAD/elements/gp.php?GROUP=starlink&FORMAT=json`) as `data/celestrak/<group>.json`, which the connector reads whenever nothing is cached; or wait for the stored retry time (shown in the health note as `waiting until HH:MMZ`). Stale cache entries are served meanwhile, and a cached group is re-requested with `If-Modified-Since`, so a `304` costs no download.
- **The earlier 403 text is gone**: each failure overwrites the message in that group's backoff row (`backoff:celestrak:<group>` in `data/cache.sqlite`), so a 403 seen earlier is replaced by whatever failed last, usually the connect timeout. The probe is the way to see what CelesTrak answers now.

## Known issues

- **CelesTrak 403**: CelesTrak answers HTTP 403 to repeated downloads of unchanged groups. The server caches each group for 3 h and, after a 403, leaves that group alone for 3 h (doubling to 12 h while it keeps failing, persisted across restarts; earlier versions retried every 30 min, which can keep a block alive). On a cold start with a blocked IP `active` and/or `starlink` may therefore be missing and only stations load. Ways out: wait; set `SPACETRACK_USER`/`SPACETRACK_PASSWORD` to get Starlink from Space-Track; or save the group's JSON from a browser (`https://celestrak.org/NORAD/elements/gp.php?GROUP=starlink&FORMAT=json`) as `data/celestrak/starlink.json`, which is used when nothing is cached. The connector reports `partial: ...` in its health note. The debris groups (`cosmos-2251-debris`, `iridium-33-debris`, `fengyun-1c-debris`) are fetched after the main three and were only tested against recorded fixtures: while CelesTrak blocks this machine they are missing, so the launch filter then has no live debris groups (it still groups any objects that share a COSPAR launch id, e.g. Starlink batches). `cosmos-1408-debris` is not offered because CelesTrak's index page does not list that group.
- **Launch groups**: derived only from the COSPAR designator, so objects without one (launches, probes, ground stations) are never in a group, and launch groups are not linked to Launch Library 2 `launch-<id>` entities (LL2 carries no COSPAR id). A group is labelled by its `A` piece, else the most common name; Starlink batches therefore show the name of one satellite. The scene fit on selection needs a handler on `runtime.onFitRequest`; until one is installed the camera does not move.
- **Horizons ephemerides**: Horizons has no ephemeris for M20 (Mars 2020) and MAVEN, so those probes have no position. The connector logs them as failed targets and retries hourly.
- **ISS telemetry items**: the item ids in the reference document were wrong (`USLAB000032/33` are not position and velocity as a pair, `S0000001`-`S0000004` are not four array angles); the table in `iss-live.ts` now follows the ISS Mimic project's list and only the items marked verified in [ISS array telemetry](#iss-array-telemetry) are claimed to be what they say. The BGA angles are shown as the feed reports them and never drive the 3D model. The wheretheiss.at fix is still the source for the live-fix marker.
- **3D models**: the models are procedural approximations built from public dimensions (no CAD): module placement, the radiators and the arm are approximate, the ISS carries no docked vehicles besides one Progress, and there is no self-shadowing or Earth-lit shading beyond one earthshine light. The ISS attitude is the measured one only when live arrays are fresh and the clock is live; otherwise (scrubbed time, mock mode, offline) the model flies the plain LVLH attitude, which differs from the real one by several degrees. A model is not drawn for objects placed on the compressed deep-space shell. The point-to-model switch is decided per entity from the camera distance, so a model is never drawn for an entity the scope policy has removed.
- **`npm audit`**: reports advisories in dev tooling only (Vite 5, Vitest 2). Upgrading means major versions and is deferred.
- **Stub connectors**: Space-Track, SatNOGS, NOTAM and webcast OCR do nothing yet.
- **Mars view**: Mars is a plain tinted sphere without a map (its position is the approximate Kepler orbit). Entities on other bodies (a geodetic provider on `MARS:fixed`, say; none ship by default) are positioned through the approximate body ephemeris and drawn as points on the Earth-centred scene. The Moon is placed with a low-precision almanac series (about 0.3 degrees, 0.5 percent in distance) and the Sun likewise; the Moon is drawn with the IAU mean rotation, so libration is not shown. Trails and paths are sampled in the active frame of reference (see Features), so an Earth satellite's trail seen from the Moon or Sun frame is the true, open relative curve (its orbit line stays the ellipse round the Earth); the compression of far probes (deep-space shell) stays Earth-centred. The Moon and the Sun, at true size, are a fraction of a pixel when the whole Earth-Moon system is in view; they are clickable with a 14 px minimum hit area (but an object under the cursor wins over a body, so LRO hides the Moon from far out), and the tabs find them too.

## Known limitations / roadmap

Implemented from `plans.md`: the two UI/UX requests (stable value layout, resizable panels), the launch filter, the camera controls, the Earth occluding the stars and trajectories for non-SGP4 objects. Performance was reported fine, including with `?mock=1`. Also implemented: not rendering objects outside the focus object's scope (SCOPE), realistic (true scale) distance projection, and the precision/clipping/resize-flicker fixes. The scene has no terrain and the downloaded Earth map is 8192 px (4.9 km per texel), so the closest views (3 km up) depend on the streamed DETAIL tiles (0.6 km per pixel at best) and are blurry beyond that. Paths show the provider's own samples: a probe whose Horizons data is missing (M20, MAVEN) has none, and Voyager-class paths are sub-pixel at shell scale (in true scale they are 4 million km long, visible when zoomed in).
